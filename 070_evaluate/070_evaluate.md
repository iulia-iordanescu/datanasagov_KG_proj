# 070_evaluate

Terms ([precision](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), [recall](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), [pair](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), pairing, [margin of error](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), [bootstrap](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), [tuning part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), …) are as defined in [docs/terminology.md](../docs/terminology.md), especially section *Evaluating extraction (step 070)*.

## Purpose

Evaluates what [step](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) 060 extracted against the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), the answer key:

- **[precision](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: when 060 says something, how often it is right;
- **[recall](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: of the ground truth triples, how many 060 found;

each with its **[margin of error](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**, so a real improvement can be told from luck. Around them:

- **[entity-class accuracy](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: of the pairs, how often 060 also named the right [entity classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas);
- **[F1](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: precision and recall in one number, the usual headline for comparing [runs](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) or [models](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline);
- **[recall upper bound](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: how much of the ground truth the [schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) can express at all (the most recall can be), and **recall [within reach](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: how well 060 did on what it could do; each also in a strict version, with the entity classes. A low upper bound means the schema needs work; a high upper bound with low [recall within reach](metrics/recall_within_reach.md) means the extraction does;
- **[describes accuracy](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: how often 060 named the right describes class, next to what always naming the most common [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) would get.

Before comparing, the [component classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) (the one 060 used) are translated into the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), through the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), which you check. Only the **[fair sample](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)** of the ground truth is evaluated, and by default only its **[tuning part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)**: the [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) is kept for the end.

The ground truth was drafted by a model (050) and corrected by a person, not written from scratch; so recall is likely overstated (see [`metrics/recall.md`](metrics/recall.md), *Assumes and can't see*). The [report](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) says so, under *Where these numbers come from*.

## To do

What to do, and when, is in [`docs/upkeep.md`](../docs/upkeep.md), one page for every step; `py run_pipeline.py --status` (or the [annotation tool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)'s home page) lists what is waiting now. For this step, in short:

- When it stops: add the [entity classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) and predicates the [finished records](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) coined to the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), and check the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)'s rows ([*When step 070 stops*](../docs/upkeep.md#when-step-070-stops)).
- After it runs: review the [partial pairs](metrics/pairs.md), then run it again; read the report's *Component class mismatches* and `per_record.md` ([*After step 070*](../docs/upkeep.md#after-step-070)).
- Look at the [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) only at the end ([*At the end: the held-out part*](../docs/upkeep.md#at-the-end-the-held-out-part)).

## How to run

From the repository folder, with the environment active (`docs/virtual_environment_setup.md`), and, when [component classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) need translating, on a computer that can reach the model's service with its key in `.env` (`docs/running_on_nasa_laptop.md`):

```
py 070_evaluate/run.py --help                       every input and setting, with its default
py 070_evaluate/run.py                              the tuning part
py 070_evaluate/run.py --evaluate_held_out true     also the held-out part (for the end; each look is logged)
py 070_evaluate/run.py --confirm_paid_calls false   don't ask (unattended runs)
```

Needs 060's output. The first [run](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) with a new [schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) stops after adding rows to `annotations/component_class_mapping.csv`: check them, then run again.

**Paying.** Before its first [model call](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), 070 logs its plan (how many component classes to translate, the [model](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)) and waits: Enter starts (the answers that go ahead, and the ones that stop it having spent nothing: `docs/running_on_nasa_laptop.md`, section 5). Any warnings were already shown, and asked about, before evaluating (stage 1), so this question doesn't repeat them. Its first call is the [test call](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), which checks that the model name and key work. A run whose answers are all in the cache never asks and never pays. Model calls, all about the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070): one per 100 component classes of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) without a row in `annotations/component_class_mapping.csv`, and at most one more when there are both `(none)` rows of the current schema and component classes of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) nothing translates to (asked once for each such two lists). None otherwise: the evaluation is code.

**The cache.** Every model answer is kept in `cache/`, under a [fingerprint](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) of everything that decided it (every part, and what isn't one: `docs/running_on_nasa_laptop.md`, section 5, *What a saved answer depends on*). A rerun reuses every answer whose fingerprint is unchanged: a failed call isn't kept, so a rerun asks only the calls that failed (but an answer cut off at the length limit is kept as cut off, and not asked again with the same settings: *A cut-off isn't paid for twice* in `docs/running_on_nasa_laptop.md`, section 5). Only an answer of the shape the step reads is kept (a `"mapping"` list for translating, a `"suggestions"` list for suggesting): one of another shape is asked once more, saying what was wrong, then counts as a failed call; one kept by an earlier version of the code is asked again, and the report's warnings name the cache file (`ask_cached` and `Cache.get` in `common/common_helpers/cache.py`). Editing a prompt asks again exactly the calls that use it; deleting `cache/` (or all of `outputs/`) means paying for every call again.

## How it works

Four [stages](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), in `070_evaluate/run.py`'s `main()`; stage 2 may ask the [model](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) (for [component classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) without a row, and for suggestions on `(none)` rows), the others are code:

1. **Pick the [records](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text)** (`records.py`, `pick_records`). A record is evaluated if you've finished it in the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) and 060's last [run](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) extracted it. Of those, only the **[fair sample](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)**: the [pool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)'s first records, up to the first one not evaluated. A record after a gap (an unfinished or unextracted pool record before it) or outside the pool is left out and named, so every evaluated record belongs to a fair sample. Every warning found here (*Checks and warnings*: ground truth problems, records left out, …) is shown before anything is evaluated, and the run asks whether to go ahead (unless `confirm_paid_calls` is false). Two problems stop the run before that, so nothing is shown or asked: [the same classed triple twice](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) in a record, and a [finished record](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) that uses an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) or predicate the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) lacks (a coined one: *The step stops*, under *Checks and warnings*).
2. **Translate component classes** (`component_classes.py`, `translate_component_classes`), through `annotations/component_class_mapping.csv`:

   ```
   kind,component_class_from_past_or_crt_schema,component_class_in_gtt,swap_subject_and_object,checked,definition_from_past_or_crt_schema,definition_in_gtt
   entity class,Satellite,Spacecraft,no,yes,A craft that orbits a body.,A vehicle in space.
   predicate,CARRIES,ABOARD,yes,yes,Has on board.,Is carried on.     "A CARRIES B" is "B ABOARD A"
   entity class,Gadget,(none),no,yes,A small device.,                 nothing in the ground truth means this
   entity class,Dataset,Dataset,no,same component class,A set of data.,A set of observations.
   ```

   For each component class of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) without a row: one equal to a component class of the ground truth schema (by [loose match](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)) gets a row at once, `checked` = `same component class` (you can still change it in the [annotation tool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), if the two definitions mean different things; it then needs checking like any other row); the model proposes the others (one [paid call](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) per 100, after the usual confirmation), `checked` = `no`. The [step](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) then stops. You check each `no` row: fix `component_class_in_gtt` (a component class of the ground truth schema, or `(none)`) and `swap_subject_and_object` where wrong, then set `checked` to `yes`. Evaluation runs only once every row it needs is checked. Each row also keeps two definitions from when it was written or last checked: the current schema's definition of its component class (`definition_from_past_or_crt_schema`), and the ground truth schema's definition of its component class of the ground truth schema (`definition_in_gtt`; empty for `(none)`). A row is **[stale](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)** when either definition has changed since. A stale row stops the step until you check it again; checking it again stores both definitions as they are then. So the one table can serve every schema, and every version of the ground truth schema: a row stays checked while both meanings stay the same. The model's proposals are only a starting point: in testing, the [stand-in model](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)'s deliberate mistake (`Phase` → `(none)` instead of `MissionPhase`) is exactly what the check is for.
3. **Compare, record by record** (`pairing.py`, `compare`). Each [extracted triple](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) is translated into a [translated extracted triple](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070). Then the occurrences in the record's multiset of translated extracted triples and its ground truth triples are paired, [exact pairs](metrics/pairs.md) first, then [partial pairs](metrics/pairs.md), as [`metrics/pairs.md`](metrics/pairs.md) defines them; for ALSO_KNOWN_AS (ground truth schema), an occurrence also pairs with its [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) and [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) swapped (*matched with subject and object swapped*, same file). Every partial pair is listed in `per_record.md`, and you review the [tuning part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)'s partial pairs in `py helpers/annotate.py`, *Partial pairs*: an occurrence and a [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) you mark `not the same fact` are never paired. Quantities are compared as quantities ([evened out](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples)), so "1 kilometer (km)" and "1 km" pair exactly, and "4.63 km" and "4630 m" too. This changed pairing on 2026-10-24: a run of 070 from before then compared quantities as words, so comparing it with a later run mixes the two ways of pairing, and some of the difference in the numbers comes from that, not from the recipe you changed. `py helpers/compare_runs.py` shows it in *What differs between the runs*, as `evaluation (070) / matching_version` (`(not recorded)` before, `2` after). The pairs gained are only those with a quantity written two ways: an exact pair where the ground truth and extraction write the same quantity with other unit spellings or units, and a partial pair where a quantity sits inside a longer subject instance or object instance.

   Which ground truth triples are [within reach](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) or [within strict reach](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070): [`metrics/recall_upper_bound.md`](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md). The [describes triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) are compared only on their [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), apart from the other [classed triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), since code writes the rest of them.
4. **Compute the metrics** (`stats.py`, `compute_metrics`). Precision, recall, F1, [entity-class accuracy](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), the [recall upper bound](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), [recall within reach](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), and the [describes accuracy](metrics/describes.md), each in its versions: [`metrics.md`](metrics.md#the-metrics).

   Each is given at both [pair levels](metrics/pairs.md) (**exact**, and **partial**, which counts exact and partial pairs), with its **[margin of error](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)** by the **[bootstrap](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)** (below), for the tuning part, for the [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) when asked, and per [stratum](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples). Precision, recall, F1, their strict versions, and reach are also given per predicate, per [subject class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), and per [object class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) (*Per predicate and per entity class*, below). The [report](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) adds a table of each record's describes class, as extracted versus as in the ground truth.

   Every number pools all the part's records' counts, never averaging records' own numbers ([`metrics.md`, *Pooled, not averaged*](metrics.md#pooled-not-averaged)). The headline (top of the report, and the pipeline report's table *Pipeline at a glance*) gives the tuning part's exact precision and recall, and how many records they pool over.

**Then the results** (`results`): `metrics.json`, `per_record.md` and `compared_triples.csv` are written, replacing the last run's (whose versions are kept in `previous_runs/<run id>/` when they differ: `helpers/audit.md`, *Where to look*); with `--evaluate_held_out true`, a line is added to `annotations/held_out_looks.csv`; the report. Each part's subsection *Metrics: … part* (section *Results*) starts with a table, *Per text*: for each evaluated record's text, how many ground truth triples it has and how many occurrences (translated extracted triples), describes triples not counted, given as the number of texts, min, max, mean, median, total, and texts with none. Each part's subsection also has a table, *Removed by 060*: for the part's evaluated records, how many items 060's checks removed, per reason as 060 wrote it in `extracted_triples_removed.csv` (the values: extracted triple in the terminology), and how many of the records had any; removed items are never evaluated, so they count neither for nor against precision, and can't pair with a ground truth triple. The table was suggested by the literature review of 2026-10-07, after Text2KGBench (Mihindukulasooriya, Tiwari, Enguix & Lata, "Text2KGBench: A Benchmark for Ontology-Driven Knowledge Graph Generation from Text", ISWC 2023, arXiv:2308.02357v1, [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02357v1), section 4): it reports the share of a model's triples whose predicate isn't in the schema, and the shares whose subject, predicate, or object is in neither the text nor the schema. The report also lists **[component class mismatches](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)** (`pairing.py`, `component_class_mismatches`), possible translation errors, found in the [tuning records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)' classed triples only, also when the held-out part is shown (a row changed because of a [held-out record](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) would let the held-out records influence the pipeline): a wrong or missing row of the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) leaves a trace where extraction found the fact but a component class differs. A mismatch of an entity class (the kinds: component class mismatch in the terminology) suggests the row of the current schema's entity class is wrong (its row may say `(none)`, or the wrong entity class); one of a predicate suggests the predicate's row is wrong (or its `swap_subject_and_object`). Each mismatch is counted, e.g. *entity class, subject Gadget (current schema) --> (none), where the ground truth triple says Device (ground truth schema), 9 times*; a single one may just be extraction choosing the wrong component class, a frequent one is a row to check first, and, if the row is right, a habit of extraction or of the ground truth. How the kinds relate, and what each counts: component class mismatch in the terminology.

The code: `070_evaluate/` holds `run.py` (the [control panel](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline): inputs, [settings](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), and the [moves](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), in order); `070_evaluate/070_evaluate_helpers/` holds `moves.py` (the moves, and writing the results), `records.py`, `component_classes.py`, `pairing.py`, `stats.py` and `readings.py` (the metrics read as sentences); `070_evaluate/070_evaluate_prompts/` holds the prompts (see *Prompts*). Shared with other steps: `common/common_helpers/ground_truth.py` (reading the ground truth; the fair sample), `common/common_helpers/text_match.py`, `common/common_helpers/triples_io.py`, `common/common_helpers/files.py` (adding lines without changing any), `common/common_helpers/cache.py`, `common/common_helpers/llm.py`.

### Reading the metrics

A percentage alone is hard to use, so for each part the [report](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) also writes every metric out as sentences, filled in with that [run](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)'s own counts (section *Reading the metrics* of the report; code: `readings.py`). It starts with **where these numbers come from**: extraction's run and [model](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), its [schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) and how many schema entries its [schema additions](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) had (naming any learned from tuning [records](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), which come from the very records being evaluated), the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) records ([pool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) positions), the partial [pairs](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) reviewed or not, for the [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) how many times it has been looked at, and where each run's full recipe is (its report's Run, [Settings](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), Prompts, and Inputs sections).

Then, per metric, the readings that hold for it, such as:

- the counts behind it, e.g. "of the 8 occurrences of these records (one per [extracted triple](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060), translated into the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)), 4 (50%) are correct at the exact level";
- read as a chance, e.g. "a [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) has a 50% chance of being found as an [exact pair](metrics/pairs.md)";
- what it means for a [knowledge graph](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) built from the extracted triples, e.g. "approximately 50% of those edges would be correct at the exact level";
- how sure it is: "for the whole catalog, exact precision is likely between 52% and 70%" (the [margin of error](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)), or, with fewer than 20 records, that it has none;
- what it assumes, e.g. that the ground truth lists every [fact](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) the records state;
- where needed, what it can't see, e.g. [entity-class accuracy](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) can't see mix-ups between [component classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) that translate to the same component class of the ground truth schema.

A metric gets only the readings that apply to it: the [recall upper bound](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), for instance, has no "chance" reading, and *describes accuracy* is read against what always naming the most common [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) would get.

### The margin of error, and the checks it needs

How it is computed, what it assumes, and how the [report](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) checks each assumption: [`070_evaluate/metrics/approximately.md`, *Sampling error*](metrics/approximately.md#sampling-error).

**Per [stratum](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)**, every stratum of the [pool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) is listed with its number of evaluated [records](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), its proportion of them, and its proportion of the pool; its numbers appear once it has 20 records. Overall numbers need no weighting, because each stratum had places in proportion to its size. Two more checks:

- the report warns when the evaluated records' mix of strata drifts from the pool's: [`metrics/approximately.md`, *The sample's mix of strata*](metrics/approximately.md#the-samples-mix-of-strata);
- with many strata, about 1 in 20 margins misses the true value by chance, so one stratum that looks unusually good or bad is not, alone, a finding. The report says so beside the table.

### Per predicate and per entity class

Precision and recall over all the [classed triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) can hide which predicates and [entity classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) extraction gets wrong. So each part's subsection *Metrics: … part* of the report (section *Results*) has three tables, under *Per predicate and per entity class*: one row per predicate, per [subject class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), and per [object class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), each with its reach (below), its occurrences, its occurrences with multiplicity > 1 (below), its ground truth triples, its pairs, and its precision, recall, and F1, and their strict versions, at both [pair levels](metrics/pairs.md) (code: `pairing.py`, `component_class_counts`, and `stats.py`, `per_component_class`). `metrics.json` has every row, under each part's `per_component_class`; the report shows the first 20 of each table. The idea comes from Negro, Futia, Kůs, and Montagna, *Knowledge Graphs and LLMs in Action* (Manning, 2026), section 5.3.3.

What counts for a row:

- an occurrence counts for its translated predicate, subject class, and object class (for a pair *matched with subject and object swapped*, the way round it was paired). A [component class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) whose row of the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) says `(none)` has a row of its own, shown as *Gadget (current schema; translates to (none))*: its occurrences can't form pairs, so its precision is 0%;
- a [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) counts for its own predicate, subject class, and object class;
- a pair counts for a predicate when both its triples have that predicate, which every pair's two triples do. It counts for a subject class (or an object class) only when both its triples have that entity class there. So a wrong entity class counts against the precision of the entity class extraction named, and against the recall of the entity class the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) has;
- a pair that counts for a row is also a [strict pair](metrics/pairs.md) for it when its two triples have the same subject class and the same object class.

*Occurrences with multiplicity > 1* counts, of a row's occurrences, those whose element of their record's multiset of [translated extracted triples](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) has multiplicity above 1: two or more [extracted triples](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) of the record translate to the same translated extracted triple ([subject instances](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) and [object instances](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) [evened out](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), component classes by [loose match](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas); code: `pairing.py`, `multiset_element`). At most one of them can pair with a given ground truth triple, so the others are extracted only unless the record has another ground truth triple they pair with (`metrics/pairs.md`, Example). The same column is in the table *Per record*, below.

Precision for a row is its pairs ÷ its occurrences; recall is its pairs ÷ its ground truth triples; F1 is 2 × its pairs ÷ (its occurrences + its ground truth triples), the formula of F1; each at the exact level ([exact pairs](metrics/pairs.md) only) and at the partial level (exact and [partial pairs](metrics/pairs.md)). Their strict versions (strict exact, strict partial) count only the row's strict pairs, as for the whole part.

*Suppose:* a record's ground truth triple is `AIRS` (Instrument) ABOARD `Aqua` (Spacecraft), and extraction's only occurrence is `AIRS` (Dataset) ABOARD `Aqua` (Spacecraft). *Then:* the two form an exact pair. ABOARD has 1 occurrence, 1 ground truth triple, and 1 exact pair: precision and recall 100%. For subject classes, the pair counts for neither row, since its two triples' subject classes differ: Dataset has 1 occurrence and no pair (precision 0%), and Instrument has 1 ground truth triple and no pair (recall 0%). For object classes, Spacecraft has 1 occurrence, 1 ground truth triple, and 1 pair: precision and recall 100%.

**Sorted by what each row costs.** Each table is sorted by *Counted against it (exact)*: the row's occurrences with no exact pair that counts for it (each counts against its precision), plus its ground truth triples with no such pair (each counts against its recall), most first. So a component class that is both common and often wrong comes first, and one seen once can cost at most 2, however bad its percentages. In the example above, Dataset and Instrument (subject classes) each cost 1, ABOARD and Spacecraft 0. The count is the one behind exact F1: (occurrences + ground truth triples) × (1 − exact F1). Sorting by F1 itself would put a row seen once and missed ahead of a common row that is often wrong, so the tables are sorted by the count.

**Reach.** Each row's *Reach* says what extraction could find for it with this translation table ([within reach](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md) and [within strict reach](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md), both defined in [recall upper bound](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md)):

- a predicate is *within reach* when a checked row of the translation table translates a predicate of the current schema to it, and *out of reach* otherwise. Within reach, every ground truth triple of the row is within reach, so the row's [recall within reach](metrics/recall_within_reach.md) equals its recall. Out of reach, none is: its recall is 0%, and its recall within reach has no value;
- an entity class (a row of the subject class or object class table) is *within reach* when a checked row translates an entity class of the current schema to it, and *out of reach* otherwise. Out of reach, no occurrence has it, so no pair counts for the row and its recall is 0%. Within reach, a pair can count for the row, but each of its ground truth triples also needs its predicate within reach, so the row's recall within reach would not be its recall. It is therefore not given; the strict one (below) is;
- *k of n ground truth triples within strict reach*: n is the row's ground truth triples, and k is how many of them are within strict reach (their predicate and both their entity classes are within reach). The column *Strict recall upper bound* is k ÷ n: the most strict recall the row can get (no value when n is 0). *Recall within reach* (strict exact, strict partial) is the row's strict pairs ÷ k: the same pairs as its strict recall, since a strict pair's ground truth triple is always within strict reach. With k = 0 it has no value, and the report says "no value (0 to divide by: no ground truth triple within strict reach)";
- a component class of the current schema whose row says `(none)` is not in the ground truth schema: its *Reach* says "none: translates to (none), so not in the ground truth schema".

*Suppose:* the checked rows of the translation table translate predicates only to ABOARD, and entity classes only to Instrument and Spacecraft, and a record's ground truth triple is `MODIS Snow Cover` (Dataset) ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT `MODIS` (Instrument). *Then:* ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT is out of reach, within strict reach 0 of 1 ground truth triple; Dataset (subject class) is out of reach, 0 of 1. Instrument (object class) is within reach, but this triple isn't within strict reach, since its predicate and its subject class aren't within reach: 0 of 1, [strict recall upper bound](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md) 0%, and its strict recall within reach has no value.

**Margins.** With 20 or more records, each of these numbers (precision, recall, F1, their strict versions, the strict recall upper bound, and strict recall within reach) has its [margin of error](metrics/approximately.md), from the same redraws of whole records as the other numbers. With fewer, the subsection repeats the warning that there is no margin. With many rows, about 1 in 20 margins misses the true value by chance, so one row that looks unusually good or bad is not, alone, a finding; the report says so beside the tables.

### Per record

Pooled numbers can hide which records extraction gets wrong. So each part's subsection *Metrics: … part* of the report has a table, *Per record*: one row per evaluated record, with its [pool position](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), the first 8 characters of its id, the first 50 characters of its title, its occurrences, its occurrences with multiplicity > 1 (as in *Per predicate and per entity class*), its ground truth triples, *Counted against it (exact)*, its [recall upper bound](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md) and [strict recall upper bound](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md), and at each [pair level](metrics/pairs.md) its pairs, its [strict pairs](metrics/pairs.md), its precision, recall, and F1 and their strict versions, its [recall within reach](metrics/recall_within_reach.md), and its strict one (code: `stats.py`, `per_record`). Each is computed by the same formula as the part's number, over the record's counts alone; *no value (0 to divide by)* where there is nothing to divide by (e.g. precision of a record with no occurrences). That is how every number of the report, `per_record.md`, and the readings below each table is written when its formula would divide by 0, with the reason after a colon where it varies (*no value (0 to divide by: no ground truth triple within strict reach)*). A record's numbers are never averaged into the part's ([`metrics.md`, *Pooled, not averaged*](metrics.md#pooled-not-averaged)). They have no [margin of error](metrics/approximately.md), since one record is too few.

The rows are sorted as in *Per predicate and per entity class*: by *Counted against it (exact)*, the record's occurrences with no [exact pair](metrics/pairs.md) (each against its precision) plus its ground truth triples with no exact pair (each against its recall), most first, then by pool position. *Suppose:* a record has 5 occurrences and 3 ground truth triples, and 2 exact pairs. *Then:* 5 + 3 − 2 × 2 = 4 count against it. The report shows the first 20 rows; `metrics.json` has every row, under each part's `per_record`, and `per_record.md` gives the same numbers at the top of each record, above its pairs, with the record's reading (*Reading each row*, below).

### Reading each row

Below each table per predicate, per [subject class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), per [object class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), and per record, a numbered list gives one line per row shown, in the table's order; `per_record.md` gives each record's line above its pairs (code: `readings.py`, `component_class_reading` and `record_reading`). The list's intro, and the end of every line, point to this section. Each line names its row (a [component class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) with its table and its schema, or a record with its [pool position](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) and the first 8 characters of its id), gives the counts it rests on, and then, for each thing that counts against the row, the steps that can cause it, among those the counts leave possible: the counts never rule out every other cause, so each line points to the full lists, in `metrics/recall.md`, *When a ground truth triple counts as not found*, and `metrics/precision.md`, *When an occurrence counts as not correct*. It uses no judgment word such as "good". The lines take the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) to be right: a wrong [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) can cause any of what they describe.

**What a line rests on.** A row per predicate or per [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) is read at the partial level, since its pairs at that level (exact and partial) are the ones that count for it: its occurrences (O), its ground truth triples (G) and how many of them are [within strict reach](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md) (k), its pairs that count for it at the partial level (P), how many of them are [exact pairs](metrics/pairs.md) (E) and how many [strict pairs](metrics/pairs.md) (S). A record is read at the exact level: its occurrences (O), its ground truth triples (G), how many of them are [within reach](metrics/recall_upper_bound.md) (W) and within strict reach (k), its exact pairs (E), how many of them are strict (S), and its exact recall and exact precision.

**Which rule a line follows**, the first that applies:

1. *A row whose [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) row says (none)* (a component class of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060)): none of its occurrences can be in a pair, so its precision is 0%. Cause (a) below.
2. *A row out of reach* (no checked row of the translation table translates a component class of the current schema to it): none of its ground truth triples can be in a pair, so its recall is 0%. Cause (b).
3. *Too few*: O + G is at most 2, and something counts against the row at its level (an occurrence or ground truth triple with no pair), or one of its pairs isn't strict. The line names no cause: it says the row has only O + G triples, and that 070 names a cause only for more than 2. The threshold 2 is a rule of thumb, with no source: at 2, one more triple would change the row's percentages by 50 points or more. A record with no occurrences and no ground truth triples reads "nothing to compare".
4. *Otherwise*, the line gives each of causes (c) to (h) below whose count is not 0, in that order. With none: "Nothing counts against it" (a row: at the partial level, every occurrence and ground truth triple in a strict pair that counts for it; a record: at the exact level, every one in a strict exact pair).

**Every cause a line can name.** The current schema is, by default, step 040's [induced schema](../docs/terminology.md#5-learning-the-schema-step-040) plus the [schema additions](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas); the translation table is `annotations/component_class_mapping.csv`; extraction is step 060's.

Any line also says how many of the row's occurrences have multiplicity > 1, when any do (*Per predicate and per entity class*, above): not a cause of its own, but at most one of those copies can pair with a given ground truth triple.

*Suppose:* a record (made up for this example) at pool position 9, whose id starts with `hypothet`, has 10 occurrences and 40 ground truth triples, all within strict reach, and 6 exact pairs, all strict. *Then:* rules 1 to 3 don't apply, and of causes (c) to (h) only (h) has a count that isn't 0: 40 − 6 = 34 ground truth triples within reach have no exact pair, and 10 − 6 = 4 occurrences have none. Its line reads "Record hypothet (pool position 9): 10 occurrences and 40 ground truth triples (40 within reach, 40 within strict reach); 6 exact pairs, 6 of them strict; exact recall 15%, exact precision 60%. 34 of its ground truth triples within reach have no exact pair, but only 4 of its occurrences lack one, so at least 30 of the record's facts within reach have no occurrence left that could pair with them: among the causes, step 060's extraction left them out, or step 060 removed the extracted triple for a check, a row of the translation table translates its predicate to the wrong one or with the wrong swap_subject_and_object, a near-repeat in the ground truth, a "not the same fact" verdict, or its occurrence taken by a wrong pair; the counts don't tell which (every cause: `070_evaluate/metrics/recall.md`, *When a ground truth triple counts as not found*). (rules: `070_evaluate/070_evaluate.md`, *Reading each row*)"

### Twins

A [twin](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) is an evaluated record with the same title, the same `notes`, or both as a record the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) was learned from. Step 030 keeps the pool and the [induction candidates](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) apart, so that 070 measures extraction on text the schema was not learned from. But two different records can carry the same `notes`: a series of datasets often repeats the same `notes` under different titles, and sometimes one title over different `notes`. The schema has then seen some or most of a twin's text, and its numbers may flatter extraction.

**In the report.** In each part's subsection *Metrics: … part* of the report (`outputs/reports/070_evaluate_<date>_<time>.md`, section *Results*), the table *Twins* names the twins by [pool position](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) and id, each with its [twin kind](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), and gives precision, recall, and F1 for all evaluated records beside the same numbers without the twins of every kind. If the two columns differ by more than their margins, report the column without the twins. `metrics.json` has the same, under each part's `twins`. The headline (top of the report, and the pipeline report's table *Pipeline at a glance*) gives the number of twins among the evaluated [tuning records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070). Steps 030 and 040 count the twins in the whole pool, in the same table (code: `common/common_helpers/ground_truth.py`, `twin_table`): 030 against every induction candidate (the most there can be), 040 against the texts it learned from as well (section *Results*, subsection *Twins in the pool*, of each step's report).

**Which records the current schema was learned from.** Step 060 writes them in `schema_used.json`, key `learned_from`:

- the [induced schema](../docs/terminology.md#5-learning-the-schema-step-040): the texts that same run of 040 learned from (`040_induce_schema/induction_evidence.json`, key `texts`), 150 with the default settings;
- the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas): pool positions 0–5, which it was written while annotating;
- either way, also any pool record a [schema entry](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)'s source line names (`source: ground truth tuning #12`).

For any other [schema file](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) (e.g. an older schema kept elsewhere), they aren't known, unless 040's `induction_evidence.json` of the same run is beside it. The report then warns, marks no record, and gives no numbers without twins.

**Twin kinds.** Each compares the pool record with one record the schema was learned from. Titles and `notes` are compared [evened out](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples); an empty one is never the same as another.

- *whole text*: the same title and the same `notes`;
- *notes only*: the same `notes`, a different title;
- *title only*: the same title, different `notes`.

A record with two kinds, with two different records, gets the one that shares more text: whole text, then notes only, then title only. *Suppose:* pool record A has the same `notes` as learned-from record X, and the same title as learned-from record Y. *Then:* A is *notes only*, since the `notes` hold more of the facts. Code: `common/common_helpers/ground_truth.py`, `twin_kinds`.

**Why these kinds, and why only the records the schema was learned from.** Two choices had to be made: which fields to compare, and against which records. Counted on 2026-10-12 (the 2026-10-01 harvest, 999 pool records), each number a count of pool records:

- Compared with all 35,407 induction candidates: 388 are whole-text twins of one of them, 129 notes-only, and 24 title-only (541 twins).
- Compared with the 150 texts 040 learns from (default settings): 3 are whole-text twins of one of them, 18 notes-only, and none title-only (21 twins).
- Compared with the records the ground truth schema was written from (pool positions 0–5): only those 6 records themselves.

Only the records the schema was learned from matter: a pool record that repeats an induction candidate 040 never read can't flatter the schema. The twin kinds say how much of a twin's text the schema saw. Most facts are stated in the `notes`: of the 150 [classed triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) of the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) and of the old [drafted batches](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) (`archive/annotations/`) that have a [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), 140 had it in the `notes` and 10 in the title. So a notes-only twin shares most of its facts' wording, and a title-only twin a few (e.g. a version, "V1.0"). Comparing whole texts alone would miss both (3 twins instead of 21, on 040's texts).

**Limits.** A `title` or `notes` that is nearly the same (one number or one word different, as in a series of datasets) doesn't make a twin. Such near-twins may flatter extraction too; nothing measures them yet.

## Audit trail

- **Prompts.** The [report](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)'s *Prompts* section lists every prompt the [run](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) filled in, each with a [fingerprint](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) (sha256) of its exact text, so the prompt behind any answer is known even if the prompt file was edited later (`_manifest.json` keeps the full fingerprints).
- **[Log](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline).** `outputs/logs/<run id>.log` records the command line, the [settings](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), the git commit, the [model call](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)'s retries (if any), each [move](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)'s duration, each output file's hash and, on failure, the full traceback.
- **[Origin](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline).** Each row of `compared_triples.csv` has an `origin` column naming the [extracted triple](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) it compares (`060_extract/extracted_triples.csv#<position>`) and the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) triple (`annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv#<position>`); the ground truth is made by a person, so a trace stops there.
- **Trace.** `py helpers/audit.py <record id>` follows a [record](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) back through every [step](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)'s output to the 010 [batch file](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) and the API request that first returned it (`helpers/audit.md`).
- **[Held-out looks](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070).** `annotations/held_out_looks.csv` keeps one line per look at the [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), in Git.

## Known limits

- **Tested on a hand-made 060 output; one real run so far.** As of 2026-09-29, 070 had been tested only with a hand-made 060 output whose right answers were worked out in advance (all 16 numbers came out as expected), and with synthetic [records](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) for the margins; 060 itself hadn't [run](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) with the real [model](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), because the model's services can't be reached from the laptop it was built on. The numbers in this guide come from those tests, not from a real run. Since 2026-10-12, the model's service is reached from the NASA laptop, where step 040 made, with the real model, the schema that 060 and 070 start from ([`findings.md`](../docs/findings.md#2026-10-12--step-040--the-baseline-schema)); 070 first ran on a real 060 output there on 2026-10-22 (its run 2026-10-22_1615 evaluated 6 [tuning records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)). That run isn't recorded in `findings.md` yet.
- **One translation per [component class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas).** A component class of the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060) translates to at most one component class of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas); if it covers two (e.g. `Instrument` (current schema) for both `Instrument` and `Sensor` (ground truth schema)), pick the closer one.
- **Partial pairing can be fooled**, and doesn't catch the same [fact](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) in different words ("the satellite" for "Aqua"), which understates precision and recall: see `metrics/pairs.md`, *Assumes and can't see*. You review the [tuning part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)'s [partial pairs](metrics/pairs.md); the [held-out part](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070)'s aren't reviewed until the pipeline is frozen for the final evaluation (looking at them earlier would mean looking at it). Before you review them there (at the final evaluation), the held-out part's partial level may count pairs that aren't the same fact. Its exact level can't be fooled this way, so read the held-out part's exact numbers as its main ones.
- **The [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) started as [drafted triples](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)**: [recall](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) is likely overstated (see [`metrics/recall.md`](metrics/recall.md), *Assumes and can't see*).
- **The threshold of 20 records** for a margin is a rule of thumb, and so is the 10 points by which a stratum's share may differ before the report warns: neither has a published source.
- **The margins of error cover sampling error only**: not the model answering differently on another run, mistakes in the ground truth, or tuning on the evaluated records (every way the numbers are approximate: [`metrics/approximately.md`](metrics/approximately.md)).
- **What the numbers can't tell apart** (a wrong row of the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) looks like an extraction mistake, among others): the full list is in [`metrics.md`](metrics.md), *Which part of the pipeline each number speaks for*. Two component classes of the current schema that translate to one can't be told apart either ([row state](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) *shared*).
- **Twins are found only by an identical title or identical `notes`**: near-identical texts aren't measured (*Twins*, above).

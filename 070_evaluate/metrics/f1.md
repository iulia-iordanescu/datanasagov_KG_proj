# F1

Part of [Evaluation metrics](../metrics.md), which explains the notation and lists every metric.

## <ins>Definition</ins>

[Precision](precision.md) and [recall](recall.md) combined into one number, high only when both are.

## <ins>Formula</ins>

Four versions, each from the precision and recall of the same version.

| Version | Formula |
|---|---|
| exact F1 | 2 × exact precision × exact recall ÷ (exact precision + exact recall) |
| partial F1 | 2 × partial precision × partial recall ÷ (partial precision + partial recall) |
| strict exact F1 | 2 × strict exact precision × strict exact recall ÷ (strict exact precision + strict exact recall) |
| strict partial F1 | 2 × strict partial precision × strict partial recall ÷ (strict partial precision + strict partial recall) |

The code computes the same number as 2 × \|[pairs](pairs.md) counted\| ÷ (\|occurrences\| + \|[ground truth triples](../../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)\|), where the pairs counted are those the version's precision and recall count. This also settles the edge cases: F1 is 0 when no pair is counted (even with no occurrences, where precision is undefined).

Each version is a number from 0 to 1; with neither occurrences nor ground truth triples across the [records](../../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), it is undefined.

## <ins>Interpretations</ins>

Let *f* be the value of one version of F1.

**What was computed** (the evaluated records)

1. *f* is the harmonic mean of that version's precision and recall: a kind of average, pulled toward the lower of the two. It is never below the lower one, never above the higher one, and never above their plain average (example: [F1: example](#example), *Pulled toward the lower*).
2. Equally, *f* is the number of pairs counted divided by the plain average of the number of occurrences and the number of ground truth triples (by the code's formula, above).
3. Why one number: precision and recall can each be fooled alone. An extractor that states just one [classed triple](../../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) it is sure of gets high precision and almost no recall; one that states everything it can think of gets high recall and low precision. *f* is high only when both are.

**What it means for the [knowledge graph](../../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline)**

No reading of its own: it combines precision's reading (how many of the graph's [edges](../../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) would be correct) with recall's (how many of the facts the records state the graph would hold). Read those two for the graph. F1 is the usual headline number for comparing [runs](../../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) or [models](../../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline), and for comparing with published results.

## <ins>Assumes and can't see</ins>

- It weighs precision and recall equally. If one matters more (for a graph, a wrong statement is often worse than a missing one), read precision and recall themselves.
- It doesn't show which of the two is low.
- Read for the whole catalog, *f* is approximate for the [reasons every metric shares](approximately.md); the [report](../../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) measures one of them, sampling error, with *f*'s [margin of error](approximately.md#sampling-error).

Worked example: [F1: example](#example). Sources: [F1: sources](#sources).

## Example

*Two records.* *Suppose:* two [records](../../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) are evaluated. Record A has 4 occurrences and 5 [ground truth triples](../../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), with 3 [exact pairs](pairs.md), 2 of them [strict](pairs.md). Record B has 1 occurrence and 2 ground truth triples, with 1 exact pair, which is strict. *Then:*

- Exact precision = (3 + 1) ÷ (4 + 1) = 80%; exact recall = (3 + 1) ÷ (5 + 2) = 57%; exact F1 = 2 × 0.80 × 0.57 ÷ (0.80 + 0.57) = 67%. By the code's formula: 2 × 4 ÷ (5 + 7) = 67%.
- Strict exact precision = 3 ÷ 5 = 60%; strict exact recall = 3 ÷ 7 = 43%; strict exact F1 = 2 × 3 ÷ (5 + 7) = 50%.

*Pulled toward the lower.* Precision 100% and recall 10% give F1 = 2 × 1.00 × 0.10 ÷ (1.00 + 0.10) = 18%, not the 55% of the plain average.

## Sources

The F-measure of van Rijsbergen, *Information Retrieval* (1979), with [precision](precision.md) and [recall](recall.md) weighted equally; Manning, Raghavan & Schütze (2008), section 8.3. The standard headline metric in relation extraction.

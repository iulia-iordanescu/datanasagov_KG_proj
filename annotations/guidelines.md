# Annotation guidelines

What to write in the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), and how, so that anyone annotating a record writes the same [classed triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples). For how to use the [annotation tool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) (`py helpers/annotate.py`), see [README.md](README.md), *Annotating ground truth: the annotation tool*. Terms: [docs/terminology.md](../docs/terminology.md).

The ground truth is the answer key extraction (step 060) is evaluated against (step 070). So every rule below is also a rule the model follows when it drafts (step 050) and extracts (step 060), and when it extracts the [triple instances](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) the schema is learned from (step 040): the shared rules in `common/common_prompts/extraction_rules.txt`. So you and the model follow the same rules. If they differed, step 070 would count the model wrong even where it followed its own rules correctly.

## Before you start

- **Work in pool order within each part, without skipping.** Make [drafted batches](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) with `py 050_annotate/run.py` ([tuning records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) by default; `--part held-out` for held-out ones) and correct them in the order of the pool: a record after a gap in its own part (tuning or held-out) isn't evaluated until the records of that part before it are done; the other part is not held back (`docs/upkeep.md`, *After step 050 drafts a batch*).
- **Read the record's text first, then its ground truth triples.** The [drafted triples](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) were written by a model: reading the text first makes it easier to see what they got wrong, and above all what it missed.
- **Annotate tuning records and [held-out records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) the same way, except that the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) is frozen for held-out records.** The tool shows each record's part. While annotating a held-out record, don't coin, rename, or merge an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) or predicate, or change a definition: if none of the ground truth schema's fits a fact, leave the fact out, and write it in `annotations/left_out.md` with a question for the experts (`annotations/questions_for_experts.md`). The tool refuses to add, rename, or merge one, or change a definition, while a held-out record is shown, and to add one a held-out record uses. Why: the held-out records measure how well the pipeline works on records it was never adjusted to, and a ground truth schema changed for them would be adjusted to them.

## What counts as a fact

1. **Only what the record states.** Never add what you know from elsewhere, and never write down what an unexplained code or abbreviation stands for. This includes its [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas): an unexplained code gets no [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) whose meaning depends on what it stands for. A code counts as explained when it follows one of these catalog conventions: "V" or "Version" followed by a number (a version); "L" or "Level" followed by a number (a processing level). Any other code needs the record's own explanation. The list of conventions is closed: a new one is added only by a decision recorded in docs/improvements.md. The record may be one part of a longer record ("part 1 of 2"); state only what this part states. A fact the record does not state in one passage may be written when a predicate's definition gives a test that the record's own statements meet (e.g. SUPERSEDES: "A record states this when it says the older one is discontinued or decommissioned and directs users to the newer one"); never infer from outside knowledge. One such test holds for every comparison: when the record says one thing is lower, higher, better (or any other comparison) without saying than what, and it names exactly two things of that kind together, the other one is what it is compared with; with three or more, no fact. The [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) of such a fact is the passages that meet the test, joined by " … ". (To see that two wordings name the same thing, you may use what the words mean: rule 23 of *Writing a classed triple*.)
2. **Every fact the record states, unless a rule leaves it out:** rules 3 to 10 below, and rules 20, 21 and 24 of *Writing a classed triple*, or it waits on an expert's answer (`annotations/questions_for_experts.md`). This holds whether or not the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) has an entity class or predicate for it. Leaving out any other fact is the worst mistake: a fact missing from the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) counts extraction wrong when it finds that fact (lower precision), and makes recall look better than it is when it misses it too; nothing else catches it. The title states facts too. *Suppose:* a title says "MODIS/Aqua Snow Cover 5-Min L2 Swath 500m V061". *Then:* among others, [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT MODIS, and HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "500m".
3. **Not the field names.** The record's text is split into its [text fields](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) (`title:`, `notes:`, …); the names are formatting, not facts.
4. **Not "is a" facts.** "MODIS is a spectroradiometer" says what type of thing MODIS is: that belongs in its entity class, not in a [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) of its own. A sentence that names one specific role or thing is not an "is a" fact, though: *Suppose:* a record says "JPL is the official Physical Oceanography Data Archive (PO.DAAC) for SST". *Then:* a ground truth triple, because it names a role only JPL holds, not a type.
5. **Not opinions.** Leave out words such as "great".
6. **Not negated facts.** A ground truth triple can't say "not". *Suppose:* a record says "Data are not available before 2001." *Then:* no ground truth triple.
7. **Not uncertain facts.** *Suppose:* a record says "The collection may include data from Terra." *Then:* no ground truth triple: the record doesn't state that it does. The same for "might", "possibly", "is expected to". A value given as approximate is stated, though: *suppose* a record says "a resolution of approximately 250 m"; *then* that is one ground truth triple, with "approximately 250 m" as its [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples).
8. **Not the values inside a data table; only what the table is about.** Some records' `notes` fields include a table of measurements. *Suppose:* a record says "Table 1 lists, for each of the 18 clusters, its redshift, position and exposure time", followed by lines such as "RDCSJ 1317+2911 0.805 13 17 21 +29 11 19 2228 I VF 111.3". *Then:* write the facts about the record's dataset (among them: it has measurements of redshift, of position, and of exposure time; it covers 18 clusters), but no ground truth triple per cell ("RDCSJ 1317+2911" has redshift "0.805"): the values are the record's dataset's own data, not facts about it.
9. **Not a fact without a specific thing.** A ground truth triple needs a subject and an object the record names. *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS captures data at a variety of spatial resolutions." *Then:* no ground truth triple, because "a variety of spatial resolutions" names no resolution.
10. **Not words that are true only as of when the record was written.** Leave out "latest", "current", "now", "then available", and words like them. Keep the rest of the fact. *Suppose:* a record says "Version 2 is the latest version." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 2", without "latest", because "latest" stops being true if another version is released. *Suppose:* a record says "V1.0 is the first version." *Then:* "V1.0" ALSO_KNOWN_AS "first version", because V1.0 stays the first version whatever is released later.

## Writing a classed triple

11. **[Subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) and [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): the record's own words, as written.** Don't paraphrase them, and don't expand an abbreviation the record leaves unexpanded. Exceptions: *What counts as a fact*, rules 5 and 10 (opinion words, and words true only as of writing, are left out); and rules 19, 22 and 23 below. A period of time or a range of values is one instance, however it is worded (rule 22).
12. **The subject instance is what the sentence is about**, which isn't always the title. A fact about the [describes triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples)'s object instance has that instance, the title, as its subject instance.
13. **The predicate reads from subject to object**, as the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) defines it (`annotations/ground_truth_schema.txt`, section PREDICATES: "Each entry below reads subject -> object"). "Dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT Instrument" means the dataset was collected by the instrument, not the other way round.
14. **[Entity classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) and predicates: the ground truth schema's**, when one fits, so records name the same things the same way. When none fits, coin one: see *Coining an entity class or predicate*, below.
15. **One fact per [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples).** A sentence that states two facts gives two ground truth triples. *Suppose:* a record says "The data were collected by MODIS aboard Terra." *Then:* two ground truth triples: [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "MODIS", and the record's dataset FROM_SPACECRAFT "Terra".
16. **Each fact once,** even if the record states it twice or in other words. Rule 23 says which wording to keep. *Suppose:* the notes say "gridded" and the title says "Polar-Gridded". *Then:* one ground truth triple, with "Polar-Gridded", by rule 23. (Two ground truth triples that are the same [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) in one record stop step 070 until one is deleted: `annotations/README.md`, *What the pipeline checks*.)
17. **[Source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text): the shortest passage that contains words for the subject, the predicate and the object; when the subject is named only in an earlier sentence, from where it is named to the end of the fact. Copied exactly.** Select it in the record's text and press **Use selection**, so it is copied exactly.
18. **A number with a unit: the object instance is the number and its unit, as written; what the number measures goes in the predicate.** *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS has a spatial resolution of 250 m." *Then:* "MODIS" HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "250 m".
19. **A qualifier that is a temporal resolution, an area the data cover, a processing level, a spatial resolution, a country, a time of day, or a target body is a ground truth triple of its own, and is taken out of the instance.** *Suppose:* a record says "This global dataset provides daily snow cover." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "snow cover", HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "daily", and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "global". *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset holds nighttime SST." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "SST" and OBSERVED_AT_TIME_OF_DAY "nighttime". *Suppose:* a record names "the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)". *Then:* the instance is "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)", and "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" BASED_IN "U.S." is a ground truth triple too. *Suppose:* a record says "The map is related to the average atomic mass on Vesta." *Then:* the instance is "average atomic mass", and the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "Vesta" is a ground truth triple of its own. The new ground truth triple's subject is the instance the qualifier was in, when that instance is the subject of a ground truth triple of its own (NASA BASED_IN "U.S."; "pixels" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "L2"). Otherwise it is the subject of the ground truth triple whose object the instance was (the dataset HAS_TARGET "Vesta").
    A word naming a kind of ground or water ("land", "ocean", "sea", "ice") stays in the instance if "the [rest] of the [word]" is true, and is an area the data cover if it is false. *Suppose:* a record says "Ocean Cloud Cover" and "Ocean Surface Temperature". *Then:* "the cloud cover of the ocean" is false, because the clouds are in the sky, so the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "Cloud Cover" and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "Ocean". "The temperature of the ocean surface" is true, so "Ocean Surface Temperature" stays whole.
    A qualifier stays in the instance, as written, if it is any other kind ("sea surface salinity" stays whole), or if taking it out would give a triple that isn't true. *Suppose:* a record says "an afternoon orbit". *Then:* taking "afternoon" out would give "orbit" OBSERVED_AT_TIME_OF_DAY "afternoon", which says the orbit comes from observations made in the afternoon. That isn't true, so the instance stays "afternoon orbit".
20. **A name with its abbreviation in parentheses is one instance, as written.** *Suppose:* a record says "This Level-2 (L2) dataset". *Then:* the instance is "Level-2 (L2)", with no ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple. Write ALSO_KNOWN_AS only when the record gives the other name in words of its own; the other ground truth triples then use one of the two wordings (rule 23). *Suppose:* a record says "The terms 'Version 61' and 'Collection 6.1' are used interchangeably in reference to this release". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 61", and "Version 61" ALSO_KNOWN_AS "Collection 6.1".
21. **General facts: include them, unless they're about [another dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) that the record's dataset isn't made from or part of.**
    - **Include** a general fact (one true beyond this record) about something the record's dataset relates to, such as its instrument, its spacecraft, or its mission. *Suppose:* a record says MODIS is aboard the Aqua satellite. *Then:* a ground truth triple.
    - **Leave out** facts about another dataset, unless the record's dataset is made from it or is part of it; for example, leave them out if the other dataset is a variant of the record's dataset (the same data at another temporal resolution, spatial resolution, or version), or is made from the record's dataset. In every case, include the connection the record states (rule 1 of *What counts as a fact*: only what the record states). *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset, A, is used to make dataset B. B covers 2001 to 2005." *Then:* "B" COMPUTED_FROM "A" is a ground truth triple, and "2001 to 2005" is left out, because it is a fact about B, which is made from the record's dataset. *Suppose:* a record says "Dataset C was used to make this dataset, A. C covers 2001 to 2005." *Then:* "A" COMPUTED_FROM "C" and "C" HAS_TIME_SPAN "2001 to 2005" are both ground truth triples, because the record's dataset is made from C. *Suppose:* a record's title says "8 Day", and its notes say "daily, weekly (8 day), and monthly products are available from this instrument's output". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "weekly (8 day)", its own (by rule 23, it says more than "8 Day"); the daily and monthly products are variants of the record's dataset, and the record states no connection beyond that, so they get none.
22. **A list joined by "and": one ground truth triple per item.** Apply rule 19 first. Then put one item alone in the list's place (the subject or the object), with the words the items share copied onto it. Each item is now one thing, so shared words that agree with the number of items are put in the singular to agree with one item ("channels" → "channel"; "are" → "is"); each item's own words stay as written ("oceans"). An item that names no specific thing ("his team") gives no ground truth triple (rule 9 of *What counts as a fact*: "Not a fact without a specific thing"). *Suppose:* a record says the SST is "derived from the long wave IR 11 and 12 micron wavelength channels". *Then:* two ground truth triples, with the objects "long wave IR 11 micron wavelength channel" and "long wave IR 12 micron wavelength channel". *Suppose:* a record says Aqua was launched "to study the global dynamics of the Earth atmosphere, land and oceans". *Then:* rule 19 first gives Aqua HAS_TARGET "Earth"; then three ground truth triples: Aqua HAS_PURPOSE "study the global dynamics of the atmosphere", "study the global dynamics of the land", and "study the global dynamics of the oceans".
    - **A period of time or a range of values is one instance, however it is worded** ("between A and B", "from A to B", "A to B", "A–B", "A through B", "A until B"). When other words come between its start and end, write the start and end joined by the record's own linking words, leaving the words in between out. *Suppose:* a record says "between 2014-11-19 and 2015-03-10". *Then:* one ground truth triple, with the object "between 2014-11-19 and 2015-03-10". *Suppose:* a record says "from January, 2007, through cruise, approach to and encounter with comet 9P/Tempel 1, and up to end of mission in March, 2011". *Then:* HAS_TIME_SPAN "from January, 2007 up to March, 2011".
    - **Lists joined by "/" or "+".** Split such a list only when the record's text also names its items separately somewhere else; otherwise keep it whole, as written. *Suppose:* a record says "Level 2/3 (EDR/RDR) data" and later "The RDR data convert the EDR (raw) sensor data". *Then:* the items are named separately, so the list splits. *Suppose:* a title says "RSI 1/2/3" and the record never names "RSI 1", "RSI 2" or "RSI 3" alone. *Then:* it stays whole. Lists joined by "and" or commas split as above.
    - **Exception: a collective "and".** A distributive "and" means "A, and also B": what the record says holds for each item alone, so the list is split. A collective "and" means "A and B together": what the record says holds only for the items together. Keep the list whole, in one ground truth triple, when it is what one of these nouns is about: "continuity", "ratio", "difference", "comparison", "correlation"; that is, the list follows the noun, as in "continuity of … A and B" or "difference between A and B". The noun must be the record's own word for how the items relate, not part of a name ("Normalized Difference Snow Index" is a name), and no other word keeps a list whole: an adjective ("combined") or a preposition alone ("between") (a period of time or a range of values is one instance; see above) does not. The list of nouns is closed: a new noun is added only by a decision recorded in [docs/improvements.md](../docs/improvements.md). *Suppose:* a record says an algorithm is "intended to provide continuity of SST derived from heritage and current NASA sensors". *Then:* the list "heritage and current NASA sensors" follows "continuity of", so one ground truth triple keeps the whole phrase: the algorithm HAS_PURPOSE "provide continuity of SST derived from heritage and current NASA sensors". *Suppose:* a record's dataset is "Combined Terra and Aqua". *Then:* "combined" is an adjective, not one of the nouns, so the list splits: the record's dataset FROM_SPACECRAFT "Terra" and FROM_SPACECRAFT "Aqua", since the data come from each spacecraft.
23. **One thing, one wording, within a record.** When a record names the same thing more than once, in different words: if one wording says more (it contains the other, is more precise, or spells out its abbreviation), use it; if they are two wordings that say the same, use the one the record uses most often (on a tie, the one it gives first); the other appears only in an ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple, and only if the record states one (rule 20). *Suppose:* a record says "generated by the NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)", and later "JPL acquires data from the OBPG". *Then:* both ground truth triples use "NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)" for their instances. *Suppose:* a title says "4km" and the notes say "4.63 km". *Then:* "4.63 km", the more precise. The record's dataset is the exception: it is always written as its title (rule 12). **Leave out a type word that only describes the thing,** written in lowercase beside the name: write "Aqua", not "Aqua satellite", since its entity class, Spacecraft, already says what it is (rule 4 of *What counts as a fact*). **Keep a type word written as part of the name,** capitalized: write "Hurricane Katrina", not "Katrina". **Keep it too when the words left once the type word is removed aren't a name:** write "GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor", not "GRaND plus Z boron loaded". A wording that differs only in capitals, spaces, hyphens or punctuation is the same wording, not a second one. Write it as it appears where the record isn't typed all in capitals (usually the notes); if it appears more than once there, the form used most often (on a tie, the first): "Vesta", not the all-capitals title's "VESTA". If it appears only in all capitals, write it as written. (These sentences are only in these guidelines, not in the model's copy of the rules: the evaluation ignores capitals, spacing, punctuation and dashes when pairing, so they change no score, and the model has fewer rules to follow.) To see that two wordings name the same thing, use clues anywhere in the record and what you know about the words (e.g. what an abbreviation stands for: MODIS is the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer; MID-IR is mid-infrared).
24. **A processing step: output = method(input).** For each processing step the record describes, find what it made (the output), what it was made from (the input), and how (the method). Each step gives up to two ground truth triples, each written only if the record names both of its ends:
    - output COMPUTED_FROM input
    - output PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM method

    One step's output can be the next step's input. A word that says only that something was done ("corrected", "calibrated", "detrended") names no method.

    *Suppose:* a record says its dataset holds "snow cover detected from the NDSI using a series of screens", and that "the NDSI is derived from radiance data". *Then:* two steps.
    - Step 1: NDSI = ?(radiance data). The method isn't named, so only "NDSI" COMPUTED_FROM "radiance data".
    - Step 2: the record's dataset = a series of screens(NDSI). This gives the record's dataset COMPUTED_FROM "NDSI", and the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "a series of screens".

    *Suppose:* a record says "The dataset's radiances were calibrated, then cloud-cleared using the CCR algorithm." *Then:* the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "CCR". "Calibrated" names no method, so it gives no triple.

### Examples

*Two facts in one sentence, and an opinion word.* *Suppose:* a record's text says "The NASA Technical Standards Program provides access to NASA developed standards and handbooks. … The idea is to make it simpler for technical people to access great NASA technical information quickly and conveniently." *Then:* "standards and handbooks" are two things, so two ground truth triples: "NASA Technical Standards Program" (Organization) PROVIDES_ACCESS_TO_DOCUMENT "NASA developed standards" (Document), and the same with "NASA developed handbooks". The two purposes ("… quickly", "… conveniently", rule 22) are written without "great". (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv`, record `3122be4c-b9d0-4cc9-b6d3-9b96c6e743da`.)

*The subject isn't always the title.* *Suppose:* a record titled "ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P RSI 1/2/3 COMET ESCORT 4 1149 V1.0" says "This is a Rosetta Radio Science data set, collected during the COMET ESCORT 4 phase 2015-10-22 to 2015-12-31." *Then:* the facts about [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) have the title as their [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): … FROM_MISSION "Rosetta" (Mission), … HAS_DISCIPLINE "Radio Science" (Discipline), … HAS_MISSION_PHASE "COMET ESCORT 4" (MissionPhase). But the dates belong to the phase, not to the record's dataset: "COMET ESCORT 4" (MissionPhase) HAS_TIME_SPAN "2015-10-22 to 2015-12-31" (Time). (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv`, record `cfd6ec3f-1e63-4a77-bc3f-007d45f7e2fc`.)

## The describes triple

Every record has one [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) that no text states, written by code: the record's id DESCRIBES its title. Your only part in it is its [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), the [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): the describes triple's [object class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) (e.g. Dataset, WebTool). Choose it in the tool's field *Object's entity class* of the [describes triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples). `X` means none is chosen yet.

## Coining an entity class or predicate

- **Coin one only when none of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)'s fits.** Check the tool's list as you type, and **Help → Ground truth schema** (in the record's strip), which shows every one at a glance: a near-synonym may already exist.
- **Spell it in the ground truth schema's style:** an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) in CamelCase (`SpaceMission`), a predicate in UPPER_SNAKE_CASE (`PART_OF_MISSION`), one word each.
- **Give it a one-line definition at once:** press the button under its ✖, in the tool. Until then the model sees it without a definition, the tool marks it ✖, and step 070 won't evaluate a [finished record](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) that uses it.
- **To rename one, or merge two that mean the same thing:** the tool's **Help → Ground truth schema**, then **Edit definitions, rename, or merge…**, then **[Rename or merge…](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)** (`annotations/README.md`, *The ground truth schema*).

## Finishing a record

- **Tick *All facts extracted*** only when every fact the record states is there. Only [finished records](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) are extracted (step 060) and evaluated (step 070).
- **A record that states no facts:** delete every [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) of the record; the tool then keeps one row of the file with only the record's id, which means "annotated, states no facts". Still tick *All facts extracted*.
- **Fix what the tool marks:** ✖ must be fixed (e.g. a [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) that isn't in the record's text); ⚑ is worth a look (e.g. a [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) that isn't in the text, which may be a typo or a deliberate rewording).
- **If a ground truth triple isn't connected to the record's id through the others,** look for the fact that connects it; add it only if the record states it. The tool marks such triples ⚑.
- **Commit the file to Git** (`annotations/ground_truth/batch_<NNN>.csv`).

# Annotation guidelines

What to write in the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), and how, so that anyone annotating a record writes the same [classed triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples). For how to use the [annotation tool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) (`py helpers/annotate.py`), see [README.md](README.md), *Annotating ground truth: the annotation tool*. Terms: [docs/terminology.md](../docs/terminology.md).

The ground truth is the answer key against which extraction (step 060) is evaluated (step 070). Every rule below is therefore also a rule that the model follows when it drafts (step 050), when it extracts (step 060), and when it extracts the [triple instances](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) from which the schema is learned (step 040). The model's copy of the rules, `common/common_prompts/extraction_rules.txt`, is built from this file by `py helpers/build_rules.py`, and `annotations/rules_model_differences.txt` lists every way in which it differs, each with its reason. If the two copies differed in any other way, step 070 would count the model wrong even where it followed its own rules correctly.

## How the rules use their words

Each wording below has one meaning in every rule.

- **Entity.** An [entity](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) is what an instance's words refer to: one particular instrument, spacecraft, mission, organization, or the like, such as MODIS. Many entities share one [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas): MODIS, VIIRS, and HIRDLS are three entities of the entity class Instrument. One entity may be named in a record by several wordings, such as "MODIS" and "Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS)", which rule 23 (one wording) writes as one instance.
- **Instance, subject instance, object instance.** An [instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) is the record's words for one entity, as rule 11 (the record's own words) and rule 23 (one wording) write them, in the subject slot of a [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) (its [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples)) or in its object slot (its [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples)). An instance is words; the entity is what they refer to.
- **Name.** A [name](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) is the record's words for one entity. A name is an instance only when it is the subject instance or the object instance of a ground truth triple: "QX7" in "QX7 Wide Field Camera" is a name, and it is not an instance when the record never says what QX7 is (rule 1, only what the record states).
- **Entity class.** An entity class is a type of entity, such as an instrument or a platform. Each subject instance and each object instance has the entity class of the entity it refers to.
- **Predicate.** A [predicate](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) is the relationship that a ground truth triple states between its subject instance and its object instance, such as "collected by" or "aboard".
- **Ground truth triple.** A fact that the record states, written as a subject instance with its entity class, a predicate, and an object instance with its entity class.
- **Qualifier, word.** A [qualifier](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) is a word or phrase, in the record's words for a subject instance or an object instance, that narrows what the instance names ("daily" in "daily lake ice extent"). Rule 19 (qualifiers) decides what happens to a qualifier. The words that other rules decide about (opinion words, type words) are called words.
- **Type word.** A type word is a word beside a name that says what type of entity the name refers to ("satellite" in "the Halden satellite"). Rule 23 (one wording), *Type words*, decides when a type word is taken out.
- **Source text.** The [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) of a ground truth triple is the passage of the record that states it, copied exactly. Rule 17 (source text) lists its cases.
- **[The title's instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), the record's dataset, another dataset.** The title's instance is the record's title, written as an instance: the record's words for the entity that the record describes. Its entity class is the [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) (*The describes triple*, below). The title's instance is called [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) when it refers to a dataset (Dataset). Every other dataset that the record names is [another dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text).
- **Standalone, describing.** An entity class is [standalone](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) when its instances exist on their own and have facts of their own, such as a dataset, an organization, a person, an instrument, a platform, a mission phase, a variable or an algorithm. An entity class is [describing](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) when its instances only describe another entity, such as a measured quantity, a purpose, a task, a role or a time. Rule 19 (qualifiers), *Whose fact it is*, uses the two.
- **Taken out, stays in the instance.** A word that a rule takes out is not written in the instance. A word that is not taken out stays in the instance, as the record writes it. Whether a word that is taken out gives a ground truth triple of its own is a separate matter, which each rule decides: a qualifier that rule 19 takes out gives one, and an opinion word that rule 5 takes out gives none.
- **Stays whole.** A name, a list or a phrase stays whole when it is written as one instance, with no word taken out, and is not split into several instances.
- **Left out, gives no ground truth triple.** A fact that a rule leaves out is not written as a ground truth triple, and the words that state it give no ground truth triple. `annotations/left_out.md` is where they are recorded.
- **States, says.** The record states a fact when its own words assert it. "A record says" only introduces the record's words in an example.

## Before you start

- **Work in pool order within each part, without skipping.** Make [drafted batches](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) with `py 050_annotate/run.py` ([tuning records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) by default; `--part held-out` for held-out ones) and correct them in the order of the pool: a record after a gap in its own part (tuning or held-out) isn't evaluated until the records of that part before it are done; the other part is not held back (`docs/upkeep.md`, *After step 050 drafts a batch*).
- **Read the record's text first, then its ground truth triples.** The [drafted triples](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) were written by a model: reading the text first makes it easier to see what they got wrong, and above all what it missed.
- **Annotate tuning records and [held-out records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) the same way: every fact (rule 2), whether or not the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) has an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) or predicate for it.** The tool shows each record's part. An entity class or predicate you coin while annotating a held-out record is added to the ground truth schema marked held-out only: a line `held-out only` under it, and a `source:` line naming the held-out records (`ground truth held-out #8`). A [held-out-only entry](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) is never shown to a model (steps 040, 050, 060, the [second opinion](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples)), is never a target of the [translation table](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070), and is never cited to shape a rule, a check, or a definition of another entry; the records that use it are evaluated as any other (its [triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) translate to nothing from the [current schema](../docs/terminology.md#6-extracting-with-a-schema-step-060), so they count as out of reach). While a held-out record is shown, you may add, redefine, rename or merge held-out-only entries only; an entry without the mark can't be touched (use the one that fits, or coin a marked one). A pattern a held-out record uses that no finished tuning record uses never becomes one of the ground truth schema's patterns (the tool flags it; if the entity classes and direction are right, keep the triple). A tuning record may not use a held-out-only entry: the tool flags it (✖) and offers **Unmark**, which drops the line and names the tuning record in the source: the entry then joins the ground truth schema every model sees, and its definition should be read again then, since it was written from a held-out record. Unmark it only when a tuning record needs the entry; the held-out records keep using it. Why: the held-out records measure how well the pipeline works on records it was never adjusted to; a definition or a rule shaped by them would adjust it to them, but a fact left out of their [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) would hide a miss (rule 2: "nothing else catches the omission").

## What counts as a fact

1. **Only what the record states.** Never add what you know from elsewhere. An unexplained code or abbreviation gives no [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) whose meaning depends on what it stands for, and what it stands for is never written, its [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) included, except as *A name the record does not explain* (below) allows. A code counts as explained when it follows one of these catalog conventions: "V" or "Version" followed by a number is a version ("V027"); "L" or "Level" followed by a number is a processing level ("L2"). Any other code needs the record's own explanation. The list of conventions is closed. The record may be one part of a longer record ("part 1 of 2"); state only what this part states. Rule 23 (one wording) allows the only other use of what you know: knowing what words mean, to see that two wordings name the same entity.
    - **A name the record does not explain.** When the record never states what a name refers to, the name gets an entity class only as this part allows. It gets its entity class, and nothing else, from a [public reference](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) that anyone can look up. Two groups of public references are allowed, tried in this order, and the first that names the entity decides. 1. An official NASA list: the Global Change Master Directory's (GCMD) Instruments, Platforms or Providers keywords, or the Planetary Data System's PDS4 context products. 2. Wikidata. The reference's entry must clearly be about the entity that the record's name refers to: the record's wording is the entry's name or one of its other names, and the entry fits what the record is about (for a NASA list, the record's own mission or platform; for Wikidata, an X-ray space telescope for a record of X-ray observations). What the entry says the entity is must fit the definition of exactly one entity class of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas): the type of entity the NASA list holds, or Wikidata's "instance of" statement and the "subclass of" statements above it. When no entry is clearly about the entity, or when the type of entity it gives fits no entity class or more than one, the name stays unexplained. The entity class is never chosen from the verb that joins the name to another instance ("observed by", "onboard"). This part supplies the entity class and nothing else: each ground truth triple must still be a fact the record states, and the name is written as the record writes it, never expanded into a long form that the record does not give. When the name gets no entity class, no ground truth triple whose meaning depends on what the name refers to is written, and the name may still stay inside a longer instance (rule 2, every fact, *A fact inside a name*). A name needs no outside knowledge in two cases. A name in the form of a personal name, one or more given names or initials followed by a surname ("R.T. Okafor"), is a person (Person) by its form. A name that contains a word saying what type of entity it refers to ("Radiometer" in "Halden Lake Ice Radiometer", "Group" in "Lake Mapping Group") is explained by the record's own words when that word fits the definition of exactly one entity class, since the word is the record's own. The reference, its entry, its address and the date it was checked go in a note of the record's section in `annotations/left_out.md`, in the item that leaves out what the name stands for.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P RSI 1/2/3 COMET ESCORT 4 1149 V1.0" never states what "RSI" is, and the PDS4 context product urn:esa:psa:context:instrument:ro.rsi is "The Radio Science Investigations (RSI) for the Rosetta Orbiter", an instrument of the Rosetta Orbiter. *Then:* the record's dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "RSI" (Instrument), with the source text "RSI"; "Radio Science Investigations" is not written.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "412 quasars that were observed by XMM-Newton", never states what XMM-Newton is, and no official NASA list names it. Wikidata's XMM-Newton (Q693861) is an instance of "space telescope", a subclass of "artificial satellite", and carries a scientific instrument. *Then:* XMM-Newton is a craft in space that carries instruments, which fits the definition of Platform and of no other entity class (an Instrument is what such a craft carries), so the record's dataset FROM_PLATFORM "XMM-Newton" (Platform). The record states the fact ("observed by XMM-Newton"); Wikidata gives the entity class and nothing else.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "MODIS/Terra Land Surface Temperature/Emissivity Daily L3 Global 1km SIN Grid V006" never states what "SIN" is, and no public reference clearly names it. *Then:* no ground truth triple has "SIN" as an instance, since it is an unexplained code, and the grid is written whole, "SIN Grid" (rule 11, the record's own words).
    - **A fact the record does not state.** A fact that the record does not state may be written only when it follows from what the record states by one of the three tests below (*Replacement*, *Comparison*, *Membership by name*); nothing else, and no outside knowledge, adds such a fact. The list of tests is closed, as the list of conventions above is. A new test or convention is added only by a decision recorded in [docs/improvements.md](../docs/improvements.md). The [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) of such a fact is the passages that meet the test (rule 17, source text, *Words in different places*).
    - **Replacement.** When the record states that an older version or product is discontinued or decommissioned and directs users to a newer one, the newer one supersedes the older one (SUPERSEDES).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The MOD11A1 Version 6 data product was decommissioned on July 31, 2023. Users are encouraged to use the MOD11A1 Version 6.1". *Then:* "Version 6.1" SUPERSEDES "Version 6".
    - **Comparison.** When the record states that one entity it names is lower, higher, better (or any other comparison) without stating what it is compared with, and names exactly two entities of the same entity class together, the other of the two is what the first is compared with. When the record names three or more such entities, no fact is written.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Two SST products can be present in these files. The first is a skin SST", and later "The SST4 product has lower uncertainty". *Then:* "SST4" HAS_LOWER_UNCERTAINTY_THAN "skin SST".
    - **Membership by name.** When the title or an identifier of [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) contains the name of a group of datasets that the record describes, the record's dataset is a member of the group. A statement about the group is then written for the record's dataset only when it holds for each member alone: it says "all" or "each", or it states a property that every member has (a version, a grid, access terms). A statement about the group as a whole is not written for the record's dataset, such as what the members cover taken together, a statement with "either … or", or how many members there are.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "BARREL 4G Rate Counter (RCNT) NaI Scintillator Diagnostics, Level 2, 4 s Data" says "The current release of all BARREL CDF data products are Version 10 files" and "BARREL observations collected near latitudes close to either the antarctic and arctic circles". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 10" ("all"; "current" is taken out by rule 10). The latitudes give no ground truth triple: "either" makes them true of the observations taken together, not of this dataset's.
2. **Every fact the record states, unless a rule leaves it out:** rule 1 (only what the record states), this rule's part *A fact inside a name*, rules 3 to 10, and rules 16, 20, 21, 22, 23 and 24 (rule 22 for an item of a list that names no specific entity and for the abbreviation of a list of several entities, rule 23 for a type word), or it waits on an expert's answer (`annotations/questions_for_experts.md`). The rule holds whether or not the ground truth schema has an entity class or predicate for the fact. Leaving out any other fact is the worst mistake: a fact missing from the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) counts extraction wrong when extraction finds that fact (lower precision), and makes recall look better than it is when extraction misses the fact too; nothing else catches the omission. The title states facts too.
    - *Suppose:* a title says "MODIS/Aqua Snow Cover 5-Min L2 Swath 500m V061", and the notes name MODIS and Aqua separately. *Then:* among others, the record's dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT MODIS, and HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "500m".
    - **A fact inside a name.** A fact counts wherever its words are, even inside a name. Every fact a name's words state is a ground truth triple, and every rule applies to it as to any other fact. The name itself stays whole (rule 11, the record's own words), and the source text of the fact is the name as written (rule 17, source text, *A name's facts*). Rule 1 (only what the record states) decides what a name's words state, as it does for all other words. A word of a name that refers to another entity, with no words that say how the two entities are related, states a fact only when the naming itself states one. The test asks which predicate the name states. That predicate must be one that the naming itself states, as decided by the entity classes of the two entities and by the naming pattern (which of the two names stands inside the other's, and where), and the ground truth schema must have exactly one such predicate. The name's words then state that predicate's fact. With the ground truth schema as it is, an instrument named in a dataset's name states that the dataset was collected by the instrument (ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT); a platform named in a dataset's name states that the dataset was collected from the platform (FROM_PLATFORM); a mission named in a dataset's name states that the dataset was collected during the mission (FROM_MISSION); a mission named in the name of one of its parts (an instrument, a craft, a campaign, a team) states that the part is a part of the mission (PART_OF); and an organization named before a group's own name states that the group is a part of the organization (PART_OF). Naming a unit with an organization's name before its own states that the unit belongs to that organization, and states neither sponsorship nor development. A relationship that naming does not state (that one entity funded, developed, provided or sponsored the other) is never read from a name, and is a fact only when the record has words that say so. When the naming states more than one predicate, or none, the name's words give no ground truth triple, since choosing one would add what the record does not state. The test decides only the facts that are read from a name's words. A fact that a sentence states with words of its own is a ground truth triple whether or not the ground truth schema has a predicate for it (rule 14, entity classes and predicates, coins one when none fits).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The NDSI is derived from radiance data". *Then:* "radiance data" HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "radiance", with the source text "radiance data", and "radiance data" stays whole.
        - *Suppose:* a record says its products "are generated by the NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)", and that a satellite "was launched by NASA". *Then:* "NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)" PART_OF "NASA", since a group named with an organization's name before its own is a part of that organization.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Using Sentinel-1 radar observations", where "Sentinel-1" is a Platform, and "The product contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data", where Copernicus is a programme that an organization runs, an Organization. *Then:* "Sentinel-1 radar observations" FROM_PLATFORM "Sentinel-1", since naming data with a Platform's name before their own states that they were collected from it, and FROM_PLATFORM ("was collected from this Platform") is the one predicate that says so. "Copernicus" gives no ground truth triple: a programme's name before "Sentinel data" could mean that the programme provided the data, funded them or collected them, and naming does not settle which.
        - *Suppose:* a record is titled "Solar Orbiter Ephemeris, …", where "Solar Orbiter" is a Platform, and no sentence of the record says how the ephemeris and the craft are related. *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VARIABLE "Solar Orbiter Ephemeris", and "Solar Orbiter" gives no ground truth triple: the fact would be read from a name's words, naming an ephemeris with a craft's name states no predicate of the ground truth schema (FROM_PLATFORM is "was collected from this Platform", which is false of an ephemeris of the craft), and the test coins none.
3. **Not the field names.** The record's text is split into its [text fields](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), each starting with its name (`title:`, `notes:`, …); the field names are formatting, not facts. A heading inside a text field, a line of its own that only names the passage below it ("Data Access"), is formatting too, and gives no ground truth triple.
    - *Suppose:* a record's text starts "title: …" and "notes: …", and its notes hold the line "BARREL Data Usage Policy" above a passage. *Then:* no ground truth triple comes from the words "title" or "notes", or from the heading.
4. **Not "is a" facts.** A sentence that says what type of entity an instance refers to ("Kestrel is an imager") gives no ground truth triple: the instance's entity class says it. A sentence that names one specific role is not an "is a" fact, though, and gives a ground truth triple ("X is the official … archive for Y"). A phrase that says what type of entity an instance refers to and also names another entity that it belongs to states two facts (rule 15, one fact per ground truth triple): the type is left out, and the belonging is a ground truth triple.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS is a spectroradiometer". *Then:* no ground truth triple: the entity class of "MODIS", Instrument, says what type of entity it refers to.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "JPL is the official Physical Oceanography Data Archive (PO.DAAC) for SST". *Then:* a ground truth triple, since it names a role only JPL holds, not a type of entity.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "Selected as a NASA Living with a Star Mission of Opportunity, BARREL was designed to …". *Then:* "BARREL" PART_OF "NASA Living with a Star"; "Mission of Opportunity" says what type of mission BARREL is, and gives no ground truth triple.
5. **Not opinions.** Take out words such as "great".
    - *Suppose:* a record says "access great NASA technical information". *Then:* the instance is "NASA technical information".
6. **Not negated facts.** A ground truth triple can't say "not", and a negated fact is not rewritten into a fact the record doesn't state.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "Data are not available before 2001." *Then:* no ground truth triple.
7. **Not facts the record doesn't assert as true:** uncertain ones, and ones it requires, requests or recommends. "May", "might", "possibly", and "is expected to" when it means "will probably" make a fact uncertain. "Should", "are requested to", "are asked to", "are encouraged to", and "are expected to" when it means "must" make it a requirement, a request or a recommendation. A value given as approximate is stated, though ("approximately 350 m" is an [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples)).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The collection may include data from Terra." *Then:* no ground truth triple: the record doesn't state that it does.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "all users are expected to read and follow the BARREL Data Usage Policy". *Then:* no ground truth triple, since the record asks it of users and doesn't state that they do.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "a resolution of approximately 250 m". *Then:* one ground truth triple, with "approximately 250 m" as its object instance.
8. **Not the values inside a data table; only what the table is about.** For a table of values inside the record (rows of measurements, one per object), write what the table is about (what it measures, what it covers), and no ground truth triple per cell: the values are the record's dataset's own data, not facts about it.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "Table 1 lists, for each of the 18 clusters, its redshift, position and exposure time", followed by lines such as "RDCSJ 1317+2911 0.805 13 17 21 +29 11 19 2228 I VF 111.3". *Then:* the facts about the record's dataset (among them: it has measurements of redshift, of position, and of exposure time; it covers 18 clusters), but no ground truth triple per cell ("RDCSJ 1317+2911" has redshift "0.805").
    - **A study a record tells.** When a record tells the study its dataset comes from, the study's aim and its method are facts of the record's dataset (its purpose, HAS_PURPOSE, and the method it was produced using, PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM, rule 24, a processing step). Nothing else in the study is a fact of the record's dataset: its background, its findings, what it says about methods it did not use, and the details of its method beyond what rule 24 writes (how the method was applied, its parameters, what it allows). A finding is a result the study drew from the data, not what the data are, and the [knowledge graph](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) that the ground truth triples build is for finding the catalog's datasets.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "In their study, the authors aimed to determine the ensemble variability properties of two serendipitously selected AGN samples … They used the structure function (SF) … They confirm a strong anti-correlation of the variability with X-ray luminosity". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_PURPOSE "determine the ensemble variability properties of two serendipitously selected samples" (rule 19 takes "AGN" out, a target body) and PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "structure function (SF)"; the anti-correlation gives no ground truth triple.
9. **Not a fact without a specific entity.** A ground truth triple needs a subject and an object the record names: "several wavelengths" names no wavelength.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS captures data at a variety of spatial resolutions." *Then:* no ground truth triple, since "a variety of spatial resolutions" names no resolution.
    - **A group named by a count or a common noun.** A fact the record states for each member of a group it names only by a count or a common noun ("40 channels each with …", "Each file is …") is written for the entity the group belongs to only when it stays true of that entity: what it holds, what it measures, what it comes from, or what format its files are in. An amount or a count per member gives no ground truth triple, unless the predicate itself says "each" (each file's period, HAS_GRANULE_DURATION; each value's spatial resolution, HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "There are 186 parameters each with a data structure that contains the following: sampling rate, units". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_ANCILLARY_DATA "sampling rate" and HAS_ANCILLARY_DATA "units", since a dataset holds what its parameters hold.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "412 quasars that were observed by XMM-Newton from 2 to 25 epochs each". *Then:* the epochs give no ground truth triple: they are a count per quasar.
    - **A word that only says how another fact holds.** Such a word gives no ground truth triple, and is taken out of the instance: its subject would be that fact, not an entity the record names. The test of rule 11 (the record's own words) sometimes keeps such a word. A word so kept is part of what the predicate says when the object is an amount (rule 18, a number with a unit), and stays in the instance otherwise.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The prime target is comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko 1 (1969 R1)". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko 1 (1969 R1)", without "prime": a prime target is a target.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The useful vertical range of the data is 1000 to 0.01 hPa." *Then:* the sentence doesn't say that the data's vertical range is 1000 to 0.01 hPa, since the data may reach beyond it, so "useful" goes in the predicate: the record's dataset HAS_VERTICAL_RANGE "1000 to 0.01 hPa", whose definition is "its values can be used over this range of heights or pressures".
    - **What data are used for.** What data have been used for, or can be used for, is a fact of the work that used them, not of the data: when the record doesn't name that work, the use gives no ground truth triple. A use that names what was made from the data gives that product's input (rule 21, general facts and other datasets).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "the data has been used to characterize the spatial scale of precipitation". *Then:* no ground truth triple, since no work that used the data is named.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset, A, is used to make dataset B." *Then:* "B" COMPUTED_FROM "A".
10. **Not words that are true only as of when the record was written.** Take out "latest", "current", "now", "then available", and words like them, and keep the rest of the fact.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "Version 2 is the latest version." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 2", without "latest", since "latest" stops being true if another version is released.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "V1.0 is the first version." *Then:* "V1.0" ALSO_KNOWN_AS "first version", since V1.0 stays the first version whatever is released later.

## Writing a classed triple

11. **[Subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) and [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): the record's own words, as written.** Don't paraphrase them, and don't expand an abbreviation the record leaves unexpanded. The exceptions are the words that rules 5 (opinions), 9 (a specific entity), 10 (words true only as of writing), 19 (qualifiers), 20 (parentheses), 22 (lists), 23 (one wording) and 24 (a processing step) take out. A word that rules 5, 9, 10 or 19 take out stays in the instance when the record doesn't state the fact without it, that is, when the record's sentence doesn't imply the fact with the word taken out. A name stays whole: rules 19, 22 and 24 never split or shorten a name ("Kestrel and Halden Survey Office" keeps its "and"; "Earth Imaging Satellites (EIS)" keeps "Earth"). A fact that the name's words state is still a [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) of its own (rule 2, every fact, *A fact inside a name*). One exception: a name made of [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text)'s code and its version does not stay whole. Each of its two parts gives a fact of its own, and the version is written as the version alone. The name of any other entity that holds a version stays whole ("version 2 Lake Archive Format (LAF2)", a file format, is one instance).
    - *Suppose:* a record says an algorithm is "intended to provide continuity of SST derived from heritage and current NASA sensors". *Then:* "current" stays in the instance, although rule 10 takes out "current": without it, the instance would no longer say that the purpose covers both the older and the newer sensors.
    - *Suppose:* a record names "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" and "Earth Observation Satellites (EOS)". *Then:* each name stays whole, the first with its "and" and the second with "Earth", and "Earth Observation Satellites (EOS)" HAS_TARGET "Earth" is a ground truth triple of its own.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The MOD11A1 Version 6 data product". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_IDENTIFIER "MOD11A1" and HAS_VERSION "Version 6".
    - *Suppose:* a record names "version 5 Hierarchical Data Format for the Earth Observing System (HDF-EOS5)", a file format. *Then:* it is one instance.
12. **The subject instance is what the sentence is about**, which isn't always the title. A fact about the entity that the title names has [the title's instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) as its subject instance.
    - *Suppose:* a record titled "ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P RSI 1/2/3 COMET ESCORT 4 1149 V1.0" says "This is a Rosetta Radio Science data set, collected during the COMET ESCORT 4 phase 2015-10-22 to 2015-12-31." *Then:* the facts about the record's dataset have the title as their subject instance: … FROM_MISSION "Rosetta" (Mission), … USES_TECHNIQUE "Radio Science" (Technique), … HAS_MISSION_PHASE "COMET ESCORT 4" (MissionPhase). But the dates belong to the phase, not to the record's dataset: "COMET ESCORT 4" (MissionPhase) HAS_TIME_SPAN "2015-10-22 to 2015-12-31" (Time). (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv`, record `cfd6ec3f-1e63-4a77-bc3f-007d45f7e2fc`.)
13. **The predicate reads from subject to object.** A ground truth triple whose subject instance is the record's dataset, whose predicate is "collected by", and whose object instance is "Kestrel" says that the dataset was collected by Kestrel, not that Kestrel was collected by the dataset. The [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) defines each predicate in this direction (`annotations/ground_truth_schema.txt`, section PREDICATES: "Each entry below reads subject -> object").
14. **[Entity classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) and predicates: the ground truth schema's**, when one fits, so that every record uses the same entity class or predicate for the same meaning. When none fits, coin one: see *Coining an entity class or predicate*, below.
15. **One fact per ground truth triple.** A sentence that states two facts gives two ground truth triples.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The data were collected by MODIS aboard Terra." *Then:* two ground truth triples: the record's dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "MODIS", and "MODIS" ABOARD "Terra".
16. **Each fact once,** even if the record states it twice or in other words. Rule 23 (one wording) says which wording to use. Two ground truth triples that are the same [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) in one record stop step 070 until one is deleted (`annotations/README.md`, *What the pipeline checks*).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset provides daily snow cover", and later "snow cover is given each day". *Then:* the two sentences state one fact, so one ground truth triple: the record's dataset HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "daily", the wording the record gives first (rule 23).
17. **[Source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), copied exactly.** 1. The shortest passage that contains words for the subject, the predicate and the object. 2. When the words for one of the three are only in another sentence, and no whole sentence lies between the two sentences, one passage from the earlier of the two (the fact, or the words for the missing one) to the end of the later. 3. Otherwise, two passages joined by " … " (*Words in different places*, below). The words for the subject or the object are the instance as written, or the words of the fact's sentence that the rules write the instance from: the same words with a word among them that a rule takes out (rule 11), or an item of a list with the words that the items share (rule 22). An abbreviation alone, the name without its abbreviation, or any other wording shorter than the instance names the entity in other words (rule 23), although its words are all in the instance: "the HO" for "Halden Office (HO)", "the radiometer" for "Kestrel radiometer". When the fact's sentence names the entity only in other words, the words for it are in another sentence, and case 2 or case 3 applies: the source text also holds the passage that shows the two wordings name one entity, usually the instance where the record spells it out. Select the passage in the record's text and press **Use selection**, so it is copied exactly; for case 3, copy the first passage so, then type " … " and the second passage after it.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The NASA Standards and Technical Assistance Resource Tool, or START, is a Web tool sponsored by the NASA Technical Standards Program. The idea is to make it simpler for technical people to access great NASA technical information quickly and conveniently". *Then:* the purpose's source text runs from "The NASA Standards and Technical Assistance Resource Tool, or START" to "conveniently" (case 2: the subject is named only in the sentence before the fact's, and no sentence lies between them).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The "HIRDLS/Aura Level 3 Geopotential Height Zonal Fourier Coefficients" version 7 data product (H3ZFCGPH) contains the entire mission (~3 years) of HIRDLS data", and three sentences later "Each file contains a zonal object with data for the entire mission". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_GRANULE_DURATION "~3 years" has the source text "the entire mission (~3 years) … Each file contains a zonal object with data for the entire mission" (case 3: the first passage has the words for the object, and the words that show "the entire mission" means "~3 years"; the second states the fact).
    - *Suppose:* a record names "the Moderate-resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) onboard the Aqua satellite", and three sentences later says "MODIS captures data in 36 spectral bands". *Then:* "Moderate-resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS)" HAS_NUMBER_OF "36 spectral bands" has the source text "Moderate-resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) … MODIS captures data in 36 spectral bands" (case 3: the fact's sentence has only the abbreviation, and the first passage is where the record spells it out).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "BARREL was designed to augment the Radiation Belt Storm Probes, RBSP, mission by providing measurements of the spatial and temporal variations of electron precipitation from the radiation belts. The RBSP mission has since been renamed the Van Allen Probes mission", and much later "The 2015 and 2016 campaigns were undertaken in coordination with the Van Allen Probes mission", and rule 23 writes the mission as "Radiation Belt Storm Probes, RBSP". *Then:* the source text of "2016 campaign" COORDINATED_WITH "Radiation Belt Storm Probes, RBSP" is two passages joined by " … " (case 3). The first runs from "Radiation Belt Storm Probes, RBSP" to "renamed the Van Allen Probes mission" and shows that the three wordings name one mission: "RBSP" alone is an abbreviation, so the passage starts where the record spells it out (case 2). The second is the later sentence, which states the fact.
    - **A title's facts.** A title's words are a list of facts of the record's dataset, also on the two sides of a "/" or "+" (rule 22, a list, *Lists joined by "/" or "+"*). A title's words give a fact only where a predicate fits what they state, by the test of rule 2 (every fact, *A fact inside a name*). The subject of each fact that the title states is the record's dataset, also when rule 19 takes the fact's words out of another instance of the title. A qualifier in the notes follows rule 19 (*Whose fact it is*). For a fact the title states by its words alone, with no words for the predicate, the source text is the title's words for the object instance. When rule 23 writes the object instance in the notes' wording, the source text must still contain the instance, so it is the title's words and the notes' words for the instance, joined by " … " in the record's order (*Words in different places*, case 1). The same holds when a sentence of the notes states the fact and rule 23 writes the instance in the title's wording. The source text is the passage that states the fact about its subject. 1. When a passage of the notes states the fact about the record's dataset itself, that passage is the source text; the title's words are joined before it only when rule 23 writes the instance in the title's wording (a wording that differs only in capitals, spaces, hyphens or punctuation is the same wording, rule 23). 2. A noun phrase of the notes that only names the instance, inside a statement about another entity, does not state the title's fact: the source text is then the title's words, joined with the notes' words when the instance is in the notes' wording, as above.
        - *Suppose:* a record is titled "Ensemble X-Ray Variability of AGN in 2XMMi-DR3", and its notes spell out "active galactic nuclei (AGN)". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "active galactic nuclei (AGN)", although rule 19 takes "AGN" out of another instance of the title.
        - *Suppose:* a title says "MODIS/Aqua Snow Cover 5-Min L2 Swath 500m V061", and the notes say "radiance data acquired by the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS)" and "observed at a resolution of 500 m". *Then:* the first passage states a fact about the radiance data, not about the record's dataset, so the record's dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS)" has the source text "MODIS … Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS)" (case 2). The second passage states the resolution of the record's dataset, so HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "500m" has the source text "observed at a resolution of 500 m" (case 1).
        - *Suppose:* a title says "MODIS/Aqua Snow Cover 5-Min L2 Swath 500m V061", and the notes name MODIS and Aqua separately. *Then:* the record's dataset FROM_PLATFORM "Aqua" has the source text "Aqua".
        - *Suppose:* a title says "VG1 J/S/SS PWS EDITED SPECTRUM ANALYZER FULL RES V1.0", and the notes say "the Plasma Wave Spectrometer (PWS) onboard Voyager 1". *Then:* the record's dataset FROM_PLATFORM "Voyager 1" has the source text "VG1 … Voyager 1".
    - **A name's facts.** For a fact a name in the notes states by its words alone, with no words for the predicate, the source text is the name as written. When the name holds only an abbreviation or another shorter wording of an entity whose instance rule 23 writes in a longer wording, the source text also holds the passage where the record writes that wording, joined by " … " in the record's order, as for a title's fact (*A title's facts*). Which facts a name states is decided by rule 2 (every fact, *A fact inside a name*). A band or channel named inside a variable's words is such a fact: the variable is for that channel (FOR_CHANNEL), and the variable stays whole.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "These data are maps of fast neutron residual count rates from the GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor". *Then:* "GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor" PART_OF "GRaND" has the source text "GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "bands 31 and 32 emissivities". *Then:* "band 31 emissivity" FOR_CHANNEL "band 31" and "band 32 emissivity" FOR_CHANNEL "band 32", each with the source text "bands 31 and 32 emissivities".
    - **A platform in an instrument's name.** In the notes, a noun phrase that names an instrument and begins with a platform's name states that the instrument is aboard the platform (ABOARD), with the phrase as source text; the instrument's instance follows rule 23 (the wording the record uses for the instrument alone, when it does; the whole name when it never does, rule 11). 1. Only a platform whose entity class comes from the record's words or from rule 1 (only what the record states, *A name the record does not explain*) counts. 2. A title's words give no such fact: they are a list of facts (*A title's facts*), which gives the record's dataset's instrument and platform, and no "aboard". 3. A "/" or "+" between the two names, in the title or the notes, is decided by rule 22 (a list, *Lists joined by "/" or "+"*), which never reads it as a name: no "aboard". 4. The instrument's name first and the platform's after it ("Kestrel Halden data") is such a list too.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "MODIS/Terra Land Surface Temperature/Emissivity Daily L3 Global 1km SIN Grid V006" says "Production of V6 Terra MODIS Land Surface Temperature and Emissivity (LST&E) data products was discontinued", and names "MODIS" alone elsewhere. *Then:* "MODIS" (Instrument) ABOARD "Terra" (Platform), with the source text "Terra MODIS"; the title's "MODIS/Terra" gives the record's dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "MODIS" and FROM_PLATFORM "Terra", and no ABOARD of its own.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P/CAL ALICE 2 ESC1 V1.0" says "acquired by the Rosetta Orbiter ALICE UV Spectrometer", and never writes "ALICE UV Spectrometer" without the platform's name. *Then:* "Rosetta Orbiter ALICE UV Spectrometer" (Instrument) ABOARD "Rosetta Orbiter" (Platform), and the name stays whole (rule 11). The title's "ALICE" is a shorter wording of the same instrument, and rule 23's test 1 picks the wording that says more.
    - **An instrument in a channel's name.** In the notes, a channel whose words begin with an instrument's name states that the channel is one of the instrument's channels (CHANNEL_OF), with the channel's words as source text (*A name's facts*); the channel's instance stays whole (rule 11), and the instrument's follows rule 23. Only an instrument whose entity class comes from the record's words or from rule 1 (only what the record states, *A name the record does not explain*) counts. A title's words give no such fact (*A title's facts*).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "VNP43D12 is the BRDF geometric parameter for VIIRS band M4 (0.555 μm)", and names "Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS)". *Then:* "VIIRS band M4 (0.555 μm)" (Channel) CHANNEL_OF "Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS)" (Instrument), with the source text "Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite (VIIRS) … VIIRS band M4 (0.555 μm)": the Channel's words, joined after the passage that spells out the abbreviation "VIIRS" (*A name's facts*). (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_004.csv`, record `0eedd2bc-b2e2-4cc1-a94d-41c141169334`.)
    - **The title's instance.** For a fact whose subject instance is the title's instance (rule 12, the subject), the words of the title need not be in the source text. A fact that a qualifier states with no words for the predicate therefore has the qualifier's words alone as its source text, the shortest passage (case 1), when its subject is the title's instance. When its subject is another instance, the passage also holds the words for that subject.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "MODIS/Aqua Snow Cover 5-Min L2 Swath 500m V061" says "This global Level-2 (L2) data set provides daily snow cover". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "snow cover" has the source text "provides daily snow cover", and HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "daily" and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "global" have the source texts "daily" and "global".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The coefficients are computed from the HIRDLS Level 2 profiles". *Then:* "HIRDLS profiles" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 2" has the source text "HIRDLS Level 2 profiles".
    - **Words in different places.** When the words for the subject, the predicate and the object are in different places, the source text is those passages, each copied exactly, joined by " … " in the record's order (every check also reads "…" or "..." between two passages, with or without spaces, as that join), in one of these three cases: 1. the title and the notes; 2. the passages that meet a test of rule 1 (only what the record states, *A fact the record does not state*); 3. two passages of the notes with a whole sentence between them (case 3 above). Each passage is the shortest that holds its words: one holds the words for whichever of the three is elsewhere (and, when the fact's sentence names it in other words, the words that show the two wordings name one entity, rule 23), and the other holds the words for the rest.
        - *Suppose:* a title says "MODIS Aqua Level 3 SST MID-IR 8 Day 4km Nighttime V2019.0", and the notes say "At night, a second SST product is generated using the mid-infrared 3.95 and 4.05 micron wavelength channels which are unique to MODIS; the SST derived from these measurements is identified as SST4". *Then:* the source text of the record's dataset HAS_VARIABLE "SST4" is the title's "SST MID-IR" and that passage of the notes, joined by " … ".
18. **A number with a unit: the object instance is the number and its unit, as written; what the number measures goes in the predicate.** A count with the noun for what is counted is such an amount: the object instance is the number and the noun, as written.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS has a spatial resolution of 250 m." *Then:* "MODIS" HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "250 m".
    - *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS captures data in 36 spectral bands". *Then:* the instrument HAS_NUMBER_OF "36 spectral bands".
19. **A qualifier that is a temporal resolution, an area the data cover, a processing level, a spatial resolution, a country, a time of day, or a target body is a ground truth triple of its own, and is taken out of the instance.**
    - *Suppose:* a record says "This global dataset provides daily snow cover." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "snow cover", HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "daily", and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "global".
    - *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset holds nighttime SST." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "SST" and OBSERVED_AT_TIME_OF_DAY "nighttime".
    - *Suppose:* a record names "the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)". *Then:* the instance is "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)", and "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" BASED_IN "U.S." is a ground truth triple too.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The map is related to the average atomic mass on Vesta." *Then:* the instance is "average atomic mass", and the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "Vesta" is a ground truth triple of its own.
    - **A processing level.** A processing level is a level the record names as one: "Level" or "L" followed by a number (rule 1), a level of a named scheme ("CODMAC level 5"), or a word the record itself calls a level ("processing level: Raw"). A qualifier that only says how far the data are processed ("raw", "edited", "calibrated", "derived") is not a processing level, and stays in the instance.
        - *Suppose:* a title says "CH1-ORB MOON M3 2 L0 RAW NEAR-IR SPECTRAL IMAGES V1.0", and the notes say "raw near-IR spectral image cubes". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "L0", and "raw" stays in "raw near-IR spectral image cubes".
    - **Whose fact it is.** The new ground truth triple's subject depends only on the entity class of the instance the qualifier was in, not on the record's other facts. 1. When that entity class is [standalone](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), the subject is that instance. 2. When that entity class is [describing](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), the subject is the subject of the ground truth triple whose object the instance was, in the sentence the qualifier is in. The ground truth schema marks each entity class as one or the other (`rule 19: standalone` or `rule 19: describing`, on a line under it). A qualifier of the "data" or "products" that a dataset "contains" or "consists of" is that dataset's fact, since those words name no other entity. It is written for the dataset also when the record names the dataset's parts and states the level of each part.
        - *Suppose:* a record names "the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)". *Then:* NASA is an Organization, a standalone entity class, so "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" BASED_IN "U.S.".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "the L2 pixels are binned into an integerized sinusoidal area grid". *Then:* "pixels" is a Dataset, a standalone entity class, so "pixels" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "L2".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The map is related to the average atomic mass on Vesta." *Then:* "average atomic mass" is a PhysicalQuantity, a describing entity class, so the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "Vesta".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "This data set contains CODMAC level 2 data". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "CODMAC level 2".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "This data set contains Level 2/3 (EDR/RDR) data" and "The RDR data convert the EDR (raw) sensor data". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 2" and HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 3" (rule 22), and the parts have the levels that the record gives them (rule 20): "EDR (raw) sensor data" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 2", and "RDR data" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 3".
    - **A country.** 1. A country that qualifies a specific person or organization (one the record names, rule 9, a specific entity) is where that person or organization is based (BASED_IN). It is a fact of that person or organization only, whatever the entity class of the instance whose words hold the country. 2. A country that qualifies what is measured is an area the data cover (HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE). 3. A country that qualifies persons or organizations the record doesn't name stays in the instance.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset holds U.S. precipitation." *Then:* "U.S." qualifies what is measured, not a Person or an Organization, so the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "precipitation" and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "U.S." (case 2). (In "the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)", it qualifies an Organization: BASED_IN, case 1.)
        - *Suppose:* a record says a working group is "dedicated to identifying and addressing Earth observation needs across U.S. civilian federal agencies". *Then:* "U.S." qualifies the agencies, which are not named, so it stays in the instance (case 3): the group HAS_PURPOSE "identifying observation needs across U.S. civilian federal agencies" ("Earth" is taken out, a target body), with no BASED_IN.
    - **Ground and water words.** A qualifier naming a type of ground or water ("land", "ocean", "sea", "ice") stays in the instance if "the [rest] of the [word]" is true, and is an area the data cover if it is false.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Ocean Cloud Cover" and "Ocean Surface Temperature". *Then:* "the cloud cover of the ocean" is false, since the clouds are in the sky, so the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "Cloud Cover" and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "Ocean". "The temperature of the ocean surface" is true, so "Ocean Surface Temperature" stays whole.
    - **What stays in the instance.** Any other qualifier stays in the instance ("glacier surface albedo" stays whole), and so does a qualifier of the seven above when taking it out would give a triple the record doesn't state (the test of rule 11). Staying in the instance decides only how the instance is written. Whether the words that stay in the instance state a fact of their own is decided by rule 2 (every fact, *A fact inside a name*).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "an afternoon orbit". *Then:* taking "afternoon" out would give "orbit" OBSERVED_AT_TIME_OF_DAY "afternoon", which says the orbit comes from observations made in the afternoon. That isn't true, so "afternoon orbit" stays whole.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Argentina's space agency, Comisión Nacional de Actividades Espaciales (CONAE)". *Then:* "Argentina's space agency" (Role) stays whole, as written, and "Comisión Nacional de Actividades Espaciales (CONAE)" BASED_IN "Argentina" is a ground truth triple too, since the words that stay in the instance state the agency's country (*A country*, case 1).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "sea surface salinity". *Then:* it stays whole, and the words "sea surface" say which salinity is measured and state no fact about another entity, so they give no ground truth triple.
20. **A name with its own abbreviation, another name or code of its own, or a short note in parentheses is one instance, as written.** An abbreviation set off by commas right after the words it abbreviates is the same as one in parentheses: one instance, as written, with its commas. It is an abbreviation only when each of its letters is a letter of those words, in the same order, and its first letter starts the first of them (the test of Schwartz and Hearst, 2003); otherwise the commas separate the items of a list (rule 22, a list). A short note is a gloss of the name: another wording of it, the entity's code, or a word that says what type of entity it refers to ("biweekly (14 day)", "RAW (unprocessed)"). Parentheses that state a fact of their own (a period, a duration, an amount, a place) are taken out of the instance, as rule 19 takes out a qualifier. The fact they state is a ground truth triple of its own unless a rule leaves it out, whether or not the ground truth schema has a predicate for it (rule 2, every fact; rule 14 coins a predicate when none fits). Parentheses that hold the code of another entity are taken out of the instance. Write that one name is also known as another (ALSO_KNOWN_AS) only when the record gives the other name in words of its own; the other ground truth triples then use one of the two wordings (rule 23, one wording). One exception, for the title's instance: rule 23 writes it as its title in every ground truth triple, so an abbreviation the notes give it in parentheses would be lost. 1. When the parenthesised short name passes the abbreviation test above, write that the title's instance is also known as the short name (ALSO_KNOWN_AS), as written and with the same entity class, with the name and its parentheses as source text. 2. When it fails the test, it is a code: the identifier of the title's instance (HAS_IDENTIFIER, Identifier).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "This Level-2 (L2) dataset". *Then:* the instance is "Level-2 (L2)", with no ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "trajectories in Heliographic, HG, Heliographic Inertial, HGI, and Solar Ecliptic, SE, Coordinates". *Then:* "HG", "HGI" and "SE" spell the words just before them, so the list has three items, and rule 22 copies "Coordinates" onto each: "Heliographic, HG, Coordinates", "Heliographic Inertial, HGI, Coordinates" and "Solar Ecliptic, SE, Coordinates", with no ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The product contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (2024-2025)". *Then:* the record's dataset COMPUTED_FROM "modified Copernicus Sentinel data", and "modified Copernicus Sentinel data" HAS_TIME_SPAN "2024-2025" (rule 21: the facts of what the record's dataset is made from); "modified" stays in the instance, as written (rule 24, *Words that name no method*).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "This data set contains Level 2/3 (EDR/RDR) data" and "The RDR data convert the EDR (raw) sensor data". *Then:* the record uses "EDR" and "RDR" as names of the data, not of the levels, so the levels are "Level 2" and "Level 3": "EDR (raw) sensor data" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 2", and "RDR data" HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL "Level 3".
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The terms 'Version 61' and 'Collection 6.1' are used interchangeably in reference to this release". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 61", and "Version 61" ALSO_KNOWN_AS "Collection 6.1".
    - *Suppose:* a record titled "OPERA Dynamic Surface Water Extent from Sentinel-1 (Version 1)" says "This dataset contains Level-3 Dynamic OPERA Surface Water Extent from Sentinel-1 (DSWx-S1) product version 1". *Then:* D, S, W, x, S and 1 are letters of "Dynamic", "Surface", "Water", "Extent" and "Sentinel-1", in that order, so the record's dataset ALSO_KNOWN_AS "DSWx-S1" (Dataset), with the source text "Dynamic OPERA Surface Water Extent from Sentinel-1 (DSWx-S1)"; every later fact of "DSWx-S1" is the record's dataset's (rule 23).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "The "HIRDLS/Aura Level 3 Geopotential Height Zonal Fourier Coefficients" version 7 data product (H3ZFCGPH)". *Then:* H3ZFCGPH fails the test, so the record's dataset HAS_IDENTIFIER "H3ZFCGPH" (Identifier).
    - **A page named, or given as an address.** 1. A page or site the record gives only as an address is a web page (WebPage), the address as written. 2. A page or site the record names in words, without a web address, is a document (Document), named as written (rule 23 takes out lowercase type words such as "project web site"). 3. A link that gives both a name and an address (`[name](address)`) gives the address, a web page, when it is a web address. When the address is a DOI, it gives the name, with the entity class that its words and the words around it give: a document only when they name a document or a page, a dataset when they name a product, a version when they name a release. A DOI is an identifier (Identifier) of the entity the link names. It is that entity's DOI (HAS_DOI) when rule 21 includes the facts of that entity, and it is left out otherwise.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "compiled by referencing several BARREL related resources including primarily the Millan et al. (2013) Space Science Reviews publication, the BARREL at Dartmouth mission web site". *Then:* the record's dataset DOCUMENTED_AT "BARREL at Dartmouth" (Document).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Details regarding methodology are available on the [VNP43MA1](https://doi.org/10.5067/VIIRS/VNP43MA1.002) product page". *Then:* the record's dataset DOCUMENTED_AT "VNP43MA1 product page" (Document); the DOI is another product's identifier (rule 21) and is left out.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The pixel temperature value is derived from the [MOD11_L2](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD11_L2.006) swath product". *Then:* the record's dataset COMPUTED_FROM "MOD11_L2" (Dataset), and "MOD11_L2" HAS_DOI "https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD11_L2.006" (Identifier), since the record's dataset is made from it (rule 21).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "please refer to the [MODIS/VIIRS Land Quality Assessment website](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/knownissue)". *Then:* the record's dataset DOCUMENTED_AT "https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/knownissue" (WebPage), and the name gives no ALSO_KNOWN_AS (the record gives it in no words of its own, above).
21. **General facts: include them; about [another dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text), only when the record links it to the record's dataset by "part of" or "made from".** A fact the record states is a ground truth triple whether or not a chain of ground truth triples connects it to the record's title (rule 2, every fact), and this rule leaves out only what its two parts name: a statement that only explains a concept, and a fact about another dataset.
    - **Include** a general fact (one true beyond this record) about an entity that the record's dataset relates to, such as its instrument, its platform, or its mission. Such an entity is named by the subject instance or the object instance of a ground truth triple of this record that is connected to the record's title (*Finishing a record*, below). A statement that only explains a concept (what a term means, how a quantity behaves in general, which methods are in common use) and is about nothing so connected gives no ground truth triple: the [knowledge graph](../docs/terminology.md#8-the-pipeline) that the ground truth triples build is for finding the catalog's datasets (rule 8, data tables).
        - *Suppose:* a record says MODIS is aboard the Aqua satellite. *Then:* "MODIS" ABOARD "Aqua" is a ground truth triple.
        - *Suppose:* a record says its coordinates "have been calculated by using the Equinox Epoch: defined via the "Mean of Date" method", and also that the First Point of Aries "drifts by about 1.4° per century", and that "Four methods for determining the Equinox Epoch are in common usage". *Then:* the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "Mean of Date". The drift and the three methods the data did not use give no ground truth triple: the First Point of Aries and those methods are the subject or object of no ground truth triple connected to the record's title.
    - **Another dataset.** Include facts about another dataset when the record states a chain of "part of" or "made from" links that connects it to the record's dataset: its parts, what it is part of, what it is made from, and so on through further datasets or variables the record names. Leave out facts about other datasets, such as a variant of the record's dataset (the same data at another temporal resolution, spatial resolution, or version), or a product made from it. In every case, include the connection the record states (rule 1, only what the record states).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset, A, is used to make dataset B. B covers 2001 to 2005." *Then:* "B" COMPUTED_FROM "A" is a ground truth triple, and "2001 to 2005" is left out, since it is a fact about B, which is made from the record's dataset.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Dataset C was used to make this dataset, A. C covers 2001 to 2005." *Then:* "A" COMPUTED_FROM "C" and "C" HAS_TIME_SPAN "2001 to 2005" are both ground truth triples, since the record's dataset is made from C.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "This data set contains Level 2/3 (EDR/RDR) data" and "The RDR data convert the EDR (raw) sensor data". *Then:* the facts about "RDR data" and "EDR (raw) sensor data" are ground truth triples, since they are parts of the record's dataset.
        - *Suppose:* a record's title says "8 Day", and its notes say "daily, weekly (8 day), and monthly products are available from this instrument's output". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "weekly (8 day)", its own (by rule 23, it says more than "8 Day"); the daily and monthly products are variants of the record's dataset, and the record states no connection beyond that, so they give no ground truth triple.
22. **A list: one ground truth triple per item.** Apply rule 19 (qualifiers) first. Then write one ground truth triple per item of the list, in which that item alone takes the list's place (the subject or the object), with the words the items share copied onto the item. Each such ground truth triple names one item, so shared words that agree with the number of items are put in the singular, to agree with one item ("channels" becomes "channel"; "are" becomes "is"); an item's own words stay as written ("rivers"). An item that names no specific entity ("his team") gives no ground truth triple (rule 9, a specific entity).
    - *Suppose:* a record says the SST is "derived from the long wave IR 11 and 12 micron wavelength channels". *Then:* two ground truth triples, with the objects "long wave IR 11 micron wavelength channel" and "long wave IR 12 micron wavelength channel".
    - *Suppose:* a record says Aqua was launched "to study the global dynamics of the Earth atmosphere, land and oceans". *Then:* rule 19 first gives Aqua HAS_TARGET "Earth"; then three ground truth triples: Aqua HAS_PURPOSE "study the global dynamics of the atmosphere", "study the global dynamics of the land", and "study the global dynamics of the oceans". "global" stays in each instance: taking it out would give Aqua HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "global", which isn't true, since Aqua is not data (rule 19, *What stays in the instance*).
    - **A period of time or a range of values is one instance, however it is worded** ("between A and B", "from A to B", "A to B", "A–B", "A through B", "A until B"). A period that the record names by its neighbouring parts joined by "and" ("March and April of 2010") is one instance too: the parts follow one another and make one period, so this "and" makes no list. When other words come between its start and end, write the start and end joined by the record's own linking words, with the words in between taken out.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "between 2014-11-19 and 2015-03-10". *Then:* one ground truth triple, with the object "between 2014-11-19 and 2015-03-10".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "from January, 2007, through cruise, approach to and encounter with comet 9P/Tempel 1, and up to end of mission in March, 2011". *Then:* HAS_TIME_SPAN "from January, 2007 up to March, 2011".
        - *Suppose:* a record says its campaigns were conducted "during the Austral summer months of January and February of 2013 and 2014". *Then:* "January and February of 2013" is one period. The two years name two separate periods, one for each campaign, so they are two items: one campaign HAS_TIME_SPAN "January and February of 2013", and the other HAS_TIME_SPAN "January and February of 2014".
    - **Lists joined by "/" or "+".** 1. In a title, a "/" or "+" always separates two items of the title's list of facts (rule 17, *A title's facts*), and never joins the words of one name. Each item gives the ground truth triple that the other rules give its words (rule 1 for a code, rule 23 for its wording). 2. In the notes, split such a list only when every item is also named alone somewhere in the record, outside the "/" or "+" phrase and not only inside another name; otherwise it stays whole, as written. An item that the title names counts as named alone (case 1). Two such lists that the record matches item by item split together. Lists joined by "and" or commas split as this rule says.
        - *Suppose:* a title says "HIRDLS/Aura Level 3 Geopotential Height 1deg Lat Zonal Fourier Coefficients V007 (H3ZFCGPH)", and the notes never name "Aura" alone. *Then:* a list (case 1): the record's dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "HIRDLS" and FROM_PLATFORM "Aura".
        - *Suppose:* a title says "ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P/CAL ALICE 2 ESC1 V1.0", and the notes say "the 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko comet escort 1 mission phase" and never explain "CAL". *Then:* a list (case 1): the item "67P" gives the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko" (rule 23, the wording that says more), and "CAL", an unexplained code, gives no ground truth triple.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Level 2/3 (EDR/RDR) data" and later "The RDR data convert the EDR (raw) sensor data". *Then:* "EDR" and "RDR" are each named alone, so "EDR/RDR" splits (case 2), and "Level 2/3", which the record matches with it item by item, splits with it.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "Aquarius L3 Weekly Polar-Gridded Sea Surface Salinity V005" says "retrievals from the Aquarius/Satélite de Aplicaciones Científicas (SAC-D) mission", and names "Satélite de Aplicaciones Científicas (SAC-D)" nowhere else. *Then:* one item is never named alone, so the list stays whole (case 2): the record's dataset FROM_MISSION "Aquarius/Satélite de Aplicaciones Científicas (SAC-D)".
    - **A list with an abbreviation of its own.** A list that the record gives one abbreviation of its own is a name, and stays whole, only when the abbreviation stands for one entity; when it stands for several entities, it is a list, and the abbreviation belongs to no item.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" and "Land Surface Temperature and Emissivity (LST&E)". *Then:* "NASA" stands for one agency, so "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" stays whole; "LST&E" stands for two quantities, a temperature and an emissivity, so the list splits into "Land Surface Temperature" and "Land Surface Emissivity", without "(LST&E)".
    - **Describing words joined by "and" or a comma.** Ask whether one of the entities the words describe (one orbit, one balloon) can have all the describing words at once. 1. Yes: they stay whole, in one ground truth triple. 2. No: split them, one ground truth triple per describing word.
        - *Suppose:* a record says Aqua was launched "into a sun synchronous, polar orbit with a daylight ascending node at 13:30". *Then:* one orbit can be sun synchronous and polar at once, so one ground truth triple: Aqua HAS_ORBIT "sun synchronous, polar orbit with a daylight ascending node at 13:30". The words after "orbit" describe the same orbit and stay in the instance, as written (rule 11); the time is not taken out, as "afternoon" is not in "an afternoon orbit" (rule 19, *What stays in the instance*).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "over 50 small, approximately 20 kg, stratospheric balloons were successively launched". *Then:* one balloon can be small, approximately 20 kg and stratospheric at once, so the count stays whole, in one ground truth triple: "over 50 small, approximately 20 kg, stratospheric balloons". The mass is an amount per balloon, and gives no ground truth triple of its own (rule 9).
        - *Suppose:* a record says a tool is meant "to access NASA technical information quickly and conveniently". *Then:* one access can be quick and convenient at once, so the phrase stays whole, in one ground truth triple.
        - *Suppose:* a record says the SST is "derived from the 11 and 12 micron channels". *Then:* one channel can't be both 11 micron and 12 micron, so two ground truth triples.
    - **Exception: a collective "and".** A distributive "and" means "A, and also B": what the record states holds for each item alone, so the list is split. A collective "and" means "A and B together": what the record states holds only for the items together (how they relate to each other, or what they make jointly); such a list stays whole, in one ground truth triple. Typical signs are a noun the list belongs to, such as "continuity of …", "ratio of …", "difference between …", "comparison of …", "correlation of …", "average of …", "merger of …". A word that is part of a name ("Relative Difference Vegetation Index") is not a sign, and an adjective ("combined", "merged") or a preposition alone ("between") does not make an "and" collective (a period of time or a range of values is one instance, as above). If what the record states is true of each item alone, the "and" is distributive. The parts of one method (its passes, steps or settings), joined by "and" inside the method's words, are a collective "and": the method is made of them jointly, and no part alone was applied as a method (rule 24, *Input or method*). "Both" is not a sign either way.
        - *Suppose:* a record says the coefficients "are computed from the HIRDLS Level 2 profiles with a Kalman filter approach using both forward and backward passes in time". *Then:* the passes are parts of one method, so one ground truth triple: PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "Kalman filter using both forward and backward passes in time" (rule 23 takes out "approach", a type word).
        - *Suppose:* a record says an algorithm is "intended to provide continuity of SST derived from heritage and current NASA sensors". *Then:* the list "heritage and current NASA sensors" follows "continuity of", so the phrase stays whole, in one ground truth triple: the algorithm HAS_PURPOSE "provide continuity of SST derived from heritage and current NASA sensors".
        - *Suppose:* a record's dataset is "Combined Terra and Aqua". *Then:* "combined" is an adjective, not a sign of a collective "and", and what the record states is true of each platform alone, so the list splits: the record's dataset FROM_PLATFORM "Terra" and FROM_PLATFORM "Aqua", since the data come from each platform.
23. **One entity, one wording, within a record.** When a record names the same entity more than once, in different words, use the wording the first of the four tests below picks, in every ground truth triple; the other wordings appear only in a ground truth triple that says one wording is also known as another (ALSO_KNOWN_AS), and only if the record states one (rule 20, parentheses). 1. The wording that says more: it contains the other's words, each as a whole word, is more precise, or spells out the other's abbreviation. When test 1 ties (one wording is more precise and the other spells out an abbreviation), use the wording that tells more about the entity, such as whose it is. 2. Otherwise, the wording from a sentence that states a fact about the entity with words for its predicate, rather than one that names it only by a title's or a name's words (rule 17, *A title's facts*, *A name's facts*). 3. Otherwise, the wording the record uses most often. 4. Otherwise, the wording the record gives first. To see that two wordings name the same entity, use clues anywhere in the record and what you know about the words (e.g. what an abbreviation stands for: VLF is very low frequency).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "generated by the NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)", and later "JPL acquires data from the OBPG". *Then:* both ground truth triples use "NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)" for their instances (test 1: it spells out the abbreviation).
    - *Suppose:* a title says "4km" and the notes say "4.63 km". *Then:* "4.63 km", the more precise (test 1).
    - *Suppose:* a title says "OPERA Dynamic Surface Water Extent from Sentinel-1", and the notes say "DSWx-S1 provides near-global geographical mapping of surface water extent over land". *Then:* neither wording contains the other, and only the notes' sentence states the measurement with words for it ("provides … mapping of"), so the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "surface water extent over land" (test 2).
    - *Suppose:* a record says "without consent of the principal investigator, PI", and later "The BARREL PI can be contacted at". *Then:* the first wording spells out the abbreviation and the second is more precise, so test 1 ties; "BARREL PI" says more, since it also says whose principal investigator the person is, and it is used.
    - *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS" in one place and "Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer" in another, and "MID-IR" in its title and "mid-infrared" in its notes. *Then:* what the words mean shows that each pair names one entity: MODIS is the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer, and MID-IR is mid-infrared.
    - **The title's instance.** The title's instance is the exception: it is always written as the title (rule 12, the subject).
    - **Type words.** 1. If the record writes a type word in lowercase beside the name at least once, outside text typed all in capitals, it is a type word everywhere in the record, even where it is capitalized. Take out a type word that only describes the entity, written in lowercase beside the name: write "Halden", not "Halden satellite", since its entity class already says what it is (rule 4, "is a" facts). The type word stays in the instance only when the words left once it is taken out aren't a name, or are the name of another entity. 2. Otherwise, the type word is part of the name, capitalized wherever the record writes it, and stays in the instance: write "Hurricane Katrina", not "Katrina". The type words of a method include "approach", "method", "technique", "procedure" and "algorithm". The type words of a dataset include "product", "data product", "data set", "data record", "catalogue" and "file". The word "data" is not a type word: it stays in the instance, as written, whatever words stand beside it.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "the GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor". *Then:* write "GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor", not "GRaND plus Z boron loaded", since the words left without "sensor" aren't a name.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The BARREL Mission was a multiple-balloon investigation" and later "the BARREL mission launched only balloons". *Then:* the lowercase "mission" shows that the record uses it as a type word, so every ground truth triple writes "BARREL" (Mission).
        - *Suppose:* a record says "with a Kalman filter approach", and another says "using a modified nonlinear SST algorithm". *Then:* "Kalman filter" is a name, so "approach" is taken out; "modified nonlinear SST" isn't a name, so "modified nonlinear SST algorithm" stays whole.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "derived from the [MOD11_L2](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD11_L2.006) swath product", another says "The RDR data convert the EDR (raw) sensor data", and a third says "available on the [VNP43MA1](https://doi.org/10.5067/VIIRS/VNP43MA1.002) product page". *Then:* "MOD11_L2" (Dataset) is written without "product"; "RDR data" and "EDR (raw) sensor data" stay whole; and "VNP43MA1 product page" (Document) stays whole, since "VNP43MA1" alone is the name of the product, not of its page.
    - **Capitals and punctuation.** A wording that differs only in capitals, spaces, hyphens or punctuation is the same wording, not a second one. 1. Write the wording as it appears where the record isn't typed all in capitals (usually the notes); if it appears there more than once, in the form used most often (on a tie, the first): "Vesta", not the all-capitals title's "VESTA". 2. Otherwise (the wording appears only in all capitals), write it as written. A leading "a", "an" or "the" is taken out: write "series of screens", not "a series of screens". (These sentences are only in these guidelines, not in the model's copy of the rules: the evaluation ignores capitals, spacing, punctuation, dashes and a leading "a", "an" or "the" when pairing, so they change no score, and the model has fewer rules to follow.)
24. **A processing step: output = method(input).** For each processing step the record describes, find what it made (the output), what it was made from (the input), and how (the method). Each step gives up to two ground truth triples, each written only if the record names both of its ends: the output computed from the input (COMPUTED_FROM), and the output produced using the method (PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM). The words for the input and the method are then taken out of every other instance written from the words for what the step made (rule 11); a name that contains them stays whole (rule 11).
    - *Suppose:* a record's notes describe its dataset as a "Multi-Angle Implementation of Atmospheric Correction (MAIAC) Land Aerosol Optical Depth gridded Level 2 product", used to calculate "the MAIAC Land Surface Bidirectional Reflectance Factor (BRF)". *Then:* the first words describe the record's dataset, whose name is its title (rule 23, *The title's instance*), so the method is taken out: the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "Multi-Angle Implementation of Atmospheric Correction (MAIAC)" and HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "Aerosol Optical Depth" ("Land" is taken out by rule 19). The second is the name of another product, made from the record's dataset, so "MAIAC Land Surface Bidirectional Reflectance Factor (BRF)" stays whole.
    - **The record's dataset's own values.** When a step's output is the record's dataset's own values, the output is the record's dataset: it is the subject of "computed from" (COMPUTED_FROM) and "produced using" (PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM), also when the record words the output as one of the dataset's variables or as its values ("the pixel values", "the ice fractions"). The same holds for a step's input: when the input is the record's dataset's own values, the input is the record's dataset, the object of "computed from", also when the record words the input as one of the dataset's variables or as its values. A variable that the record tells apart from the dataset's other variables, with a step of its own, is the output of that step, and it is the input of each later step that the record describes for it.
        - *Suppose:* a record titled "HIRDLS/Aura Level 3 Geopotential Height 1deg Lat Zonal Fourier Coefficients V007 (H3ZFCGPH)" says "The coefficients are computed from the HIRDLS Level 2 profiles with a Kalman filter approach using both forward and backward passes in time", and later "The precision values are given by the root-mean square of the differences between the estimated fields and the input data". *Then:* the coefficients are the dataset's own values, so the record's dataset COMPUTED_FROM "HIRDLS profiles" and PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "Kalman filter using both forward and backward passes in time". The precision values are one Variable among the dataset's, with a step of their own, so "precision values" PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "root-mean square".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "VNP43D12 is the BRDF geometric parameter for VIIRS band M4 (0.555 μm). The geometric parameter, in conjunction with the isotropic and volumetric parameters, is used to derive the BRDF/Albedo values for VIIRS band M4." *Then:* the geometric parameter is the record's dataset's own values, and the record describes no step that made it, so "BRDF/Albedo values for VIIRS band M4" COMPUTED_FROM the record's dataset.
    - **One step after another.** One step's output can be the next step's input.
        - *Suppose:* a record says its dataset holds "snow cover detected from the NDSI using a series of screens", and that "the NDSI is derived from radiance data". *Then:* two steps. Step 1: NDSI = ?(radiance data); the method isn't named, so only "NDSI" COMPUTED_FROM "radiance data". Step 2: the record's dataset = series of screens(NDSI); this gives the record's dataset COMPUTED_FROM "NDSI", and the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "series of screens" (rule 23: a leading "a" is taken out). The record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "snow cover", without the words for the input and the method.
    - **Words that name no method.** A method is named by a noun or a name in the method's place of output = method(input): the record states that the output was made by it, is stored as it, or is given by it ("stored as 10 s sums of counts", "Mean weekly … products", "given by the standard deviation of the differences", "using a modified two-band ice algorithm"). 1. Such a noun or name gives the output produced using the method (PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM), as written. 2. A verb or participle that says only what was done to the data ("corrected", "calibrated", "detrended", "binned", "mapped", "edited", "convert", "weighted") names no method: it gives no "produced using" ground truth triple, and stays in the instance, as written (rule 19, *What stays in the instance*). Another fact that such a word states (the form of the data, their grid) is still a ground truth triple (rule 2, every fact). 3. A noun for a statistic that names a stored field, not how the output was made ("given as a mean and three harmonics"), is a variable, not a method. What a step removed from the data, or corrected in them, is neither the step's input nor its method: the step's output is cleaned of it (CLEANED_OF), when the record names it, and the record's words also stay in the instance, as written.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The rate count values are stored as 4 s accumulations of counts" and "The nominal fast neutron count rates were detrended using the GRaND epithermal neutron map". *Then:* the first gives the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "accumulations" (and HAS_COMPOSITING_PERIOD "4 s", rule 18); the second gives "detrended" no PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM triple, and the map's product COMPUTED_FROM "GRaND epithermal neutron map".
        - *Suppose:* a record says its dataset is a "gridded Level 2 product". *Then:* "gridded" names no method, and the form it states gives HAS_SPATIAL_REPRESENTATION "gridded".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Expressed as the mean and up to 7 sine and cosine coefficients". *Then:* the mean and the coefficients are Variables, not methods.
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The dataset's radiances were calibrated, then cloud-cleared using the CCR algorithm." *Then:* the record's dataset PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "CCR"; "calibrated" names no method, so it gives no PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM triple.
        - *Suppose:* a record says its dataset holds "calibrated radiances". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "calibrated radiances".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "Some noise lines during the planetary encounters have been edited from the data set." *Then:* the record's dataset CLEANED_OF "noise lines during the planetary encounters".
        - *Suppose:* a record says its dataset holds "solar stray-light corrected … image data". *Then:* the record's dataset CLEANED_OF "solar stray-light", and the words "solar stray-light corrected" stay in the instance.
    - **Input or method.** A method is what could be applied again to other data (an algorithm, a procedure, and its settings, such as coefficients tuned per month); an input is the data the step was applied to, which its output is made from. The test is the W3C sensor ontology's (SOSA/SSN): a procedure "is re-usable, and might be involved in many Observations", and an input is "information that is provided to a Procedure for its use". The statistics the CF Conventions' cell_methods name (sum or accumulation, mean, maximum, minimum, median, mode, range, standard deviation, variance, root mean square; CF 1.11, Appendix E) are methods in this sense. An amount of data ("10 days of data", "3 years of observations") says how much data, not which: the amount is a number with its unit (rule 18), and the rest is an input only if it names data beyond the word "data".
        - *Suppose:* a record says "The NDSI is derived from radiance data". *Then:* "radiance data" is an input, since the NDSI is made from them and they are not applied to other data: "NDSI" COMPUTED_FROM "radiance data". "Multi-Angle Implementation of Atmospheric Correction (MAIAC)", in this rule's first example, is a method: it is applied again to other data.
        - *Suppose:* a record says its product "is produced daily using 16 days of data". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "daily" and HAS_COMPOSITING_PERIOD "16 days", and no COMPUTED_FROM.

### Examples

*Two facts in one sentence, and an opinion word.* *Suppose:* a record's text says "The NASA Technical Standards Program provides access to NASA developed standards and handbooks. … The idea is to make it simpler for technical people to access great NASA technical information quickly and conveniently." *Then:* "standards and handbooks" name two entities, so two ground truth triples: "NASA Technical Standards Program" (Organization) PROVIDES_ACCESS_TO_DOCUMENT "NASA developed standards" (Document), and the same with "NASA developed handbooks". The purpose is one [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), "make it simpler for technical people to access NASA technical information quickly and conveniently" (rule 22: "quickly and conveniently" describe one action), written without "great". (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv`, record `3122be4c-b9d0-4cc9-b6d3-9b96c6e743da`.)

## The describes triple

Every record has one [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) that no text states, written by code: the record's id DESCRIBES its title. Your only part in it is its [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), the [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): the describes triple's [object class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) (e.g. Dataset, WebTool). Choose it in the tool's field *Object's entity class* of the [describes triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples). `X` means none is chosen yet.

## Coining an entity class or predicate

- **Coin one only when none of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)'s fits.** Check the tool's list as you type, and **Help → Ground truth schema** (in the record's strip), which shows every one at a glance: a near-synonym may already exist.
- **Spell it in the ground truth schema's style:** an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) in CamelCase (`SpaceMission`), a predicate in UPPER_SNAKE_CASE (`PART_OF_MISSION`), one word each.
- **Mark a new entity class for rule 19:** a line `rule 19: standalone` or `rule 19: describing` under it in `annotations/ground_truth_schema.txt` (rule 19 of *Writing a classed triple*, *Whose fact it is*). The tool doesn't add it; the tests name every entity class without one.
- **Give it a one-line definition at once:** press the button under its ✖, in the tool. Until then the model sees it without a definition, the tool marks it ✖, and step 070 won't evaluate a [finished record](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) that uses it.
- **To rename one, or merge two that have the same meaning:** the tool's **Help → Ground truth schema**, then **Edit definitions, rename, or merge…**, then **[Rename or merge…](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)** (`annotations/README.md`, *The ground truth schema*).

## Finishing a record

- **Tick *All facts extracted*** only when every fact the record states is there. Only [finished records](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) are extracted (step 060) and evaluated (step 070). The tool lets it be ticked only once no row of the record has a ✖ (step 070 won't evaluate a finished record with one).
- **A record that states no facts:** delete every [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) of the record; the tool then keeps one row of the file with only the record's id, which means "annotated, states no facts". Still tick *All facts extracted*.
- **Fix what the tool marks:** ✖ must be fixed (e.g. a [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) that isn't in the record's text); ⚑ is worth a look (e.g. a [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) that isn't in the text, which may be a typo or a deliberate rewording).
- **Record the rules each ground truth triple uses** (*Rules*, under it in the tool): the numbers of the rules of this file that decided its wording. Rules 2, 11, 13, 14 and 17 apply to every ground truth triple and aren't recorded; the [describes triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) follows *The describes triple* instead. `rules_by_triple.md` lists them by record, so a rule whose wording changes names the ground truth triples to check again.
- **A record that contradicts itself** (two passages disagree about one fact, such as a title that names one spacecraft and notes about another body): write what each passage states (rule 1 of *What counts as a fact*), and correct neither from outside knowledge. Add an item to `annotations/left_out.md` that quotes both passages, so that the conflict can be reported to the record's maintainer. *Suppose:* a record is titled "Solar Orbiter Ephemeris, …", and its notes say "Heliocentric trajectories for Comet Borrelly". *Then:* [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) HAS_VARIABLE "Solar Orbiter Ephemeris" and HAS_TARGET "Comet Borrelly", and an item of `annotations/left_out.md` quotes both.
- **If a ground truth triple isn't connected, through the others, to the record's title (which the describes triple connects to the record's id),** look for the fact that connects the [triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples), and add that fact only if the record states it. The triple is kept in either case (rule 21 of *Writing a classed triple*). The tool marks such triples ⚑.
- **Commit the file to Git** (`annotations/ground_truth/batch_<NNN>.csv`).

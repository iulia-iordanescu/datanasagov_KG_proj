# Annotation guidelines

What to write in the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), and how, so that anyone annotating a record writes the same [classed triples](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples). For how to use the [annotation tool](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) (`py helpers/annotate.py`), see [README.md](README.md), *Annotating ground truth: the annotation tool*. Terms: [docs/terminology.md](../docs/terminology.md).

The ground truth is the answer key extraction (step 060) is evaluated against (step 070). So every rule below is also a rule the model follows when it drafts (step 050) and extracts (step 060), and when it extracts the [triple instances](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) the schema is learned from (step 040): the shared rules in `common/common_prompts/extraction_rules.txt`. So you and the model follow the same rules. If they differed, step 070 would count the model wrong even where it followed its own rules correctly.

## Before you start

- **Work in pool order within each part, without skipping.** Make [drafted batches](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) with `py 050_annotate/run.py` ([tuning records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) by default; `--part held-out` for held-out ones) and correct them in the order of the pool: a record after a gap in its own part (tuning or held-out) isn't evaluated until the records of that part before it are done; the other part is not held back (`docs/upkeep.md`, *After step 050 drafts a batch*).
- **Read the record's text first, then its ground truth triples.** The [drafted triples](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) were written by a model: reading the text first makes it easier to see what they got wrong, and above all what it missed.
- **Annotate tuning records and [held-out records](../docs/terminology.md#7-evaluating-extraction-step-070) the same way.** The tool shows each record's part; it changes nothing about how you annotate.

## What counts as a fact

1. **Only what the record states.** Never add what you know from elsewhere, and never write down what an unexplained code or acronym stands for. (To see that two wordings name the same thing, you may use what the words mean: rule 13 of *Writing a classed triple*.)
2. **Every fact the record states, unless a rule leaves it out:** rules 3 to 10 below, and rules 10, 11 and 14 of *Writing a classed triple*, or it waits on an expert's answer (`annotations/questions_for_experts.md`). This holds whether or not the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) has an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) or predicate for it. Leaving out any other fact is the worst mistake: a fact missing from the [ground truth](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) counts extraction wrong when it finds that fact (lower precision), and makes recall look better than it is when it misses it too; nothing else catches it. The title states facts too. *Suppose:* a title says "MODIS/Aqua Snow Cover 5-Min L2 Swath 500m V061". *Then:* among others, [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT MODIS, and HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "500m".
3. **Not the field names.** The record's text is split into its [text fields](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) (`title:`, `notes:`, …); the names are formatting, not facts.
4. **Not "is a" facts.** "MODIS is a spectroradiometer" says what type of thing MODIS is: that belongs in its entity class, not in a [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) of its own. A sentence that names one specific role or thing is not an "is a" fact, though: *Suppose:* a record says "JPL is the official Physical Oceanography Data Archive (PO.DAAC) for SST". *Then:* a [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples), because it names a role only JPL holds, not a type.
5. **Not opinions.** Leave out words such as "great".
6. **Not negated facts.** A ground truth triple can't say "not". *Suppose:* a record says "Data are not available before 2001." *Then:* no ground truth triple.
7. **Not uncertain facts.** *Suppose:* a record says "The collection may include data from Terra." *Then:* no ground truth triple: the record doesn't state that it does. The same for "might", "possibly", "is expected to". A value given as approximate is stated, though: *suppose* a record says "a resolution of approximately 250 m"; *then* that is one ground truth triple, with "approximately 250 m" as its [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples).
8. **Not the values inside a data table; only what the table is about.** Some records' `notes` fields include a table of measurements. *Suppose:* a record says "Table 1 lists, for each of the 18 clusters, its redshift, position and exposure time", followed by lines such as "RDCSJ 1317+2911 0.805 13 17 21 +29 11 19 2228 I VF 111.3". *Then:* write the facts about the record's dataset (among them: it has measurements of redshift, of position, and of exposure time; it covers 18 clusters), but no ground truth triple per cell ("RDCSJ 1317+2911" has redshift "0.805"): the values are the record's dataset's own data, not facts about it.
9. **Not a fact without a specific thing.** A ground truth triple needs a subject and an object the record names. *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS captures data at a variety of spatial resolutions." *Then:* no ground truth triple, because "a variety of spatial resolutions" names no resolution.
10. **Not words that are true only as of when the record was written.** Leave out "latest", "current", "now", "then available", and words like them. Keep the rest of the fact. *Suppose:* a record says "Version 2 is the latest version." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 2", without "latest", because "latest" stops being true if another version is released. *Suppose:* a record says "V1.0 is the first version." *Then:* "V1.0" ALSO_KNOWN_AS "first version", because V1.0 stays the first version whatever is released later.

## Writing a classed triple

1. **[Subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) and [object instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): the record's own words, as written.** Don't paraphrase them, and don't expand an acronym the record leaves unexpanded. Three exceptions: rule 9 takes some [qualifiers](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) out of an instance, rule 12 copies a list's shared words onto each item, and rule 13 uses one wording for a thing everywhere in the record.
2. **The subject instance is what the sentence is about**, which isn't always the title. Where the record states a fact about what its title names, use the title as the subject instance.
3. **The predicate reads from subject to object**, as the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) defines it (`annotations/ground_truth_schema.txt`, section PREDICATES: "Each entry below reads subject -> object"). "Dataset ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT Instrument" means the dataset was collected by the instrument, not the other way round.
4. **[Entity classes](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) and predicates: the ground truth schema's**, when one fits, so records name the same things the same way. When none fits, coin one: see *Coining an entity class or predicate*, below.
5. **One fact per [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples).** A sentence that states two facts gives two ground truth triples. *Suppose:* a record says "The data were collected by MODIS aboard Terra." *Then:* two ground truth triples: [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) ACQUIRED_BY_INSTRUMENT "MODIS", and the record's dataset FROM_SPACECRAFT "Terra".
6. **Each fact once,** even if the record states it twice or in other words. Rule 13 says which wording to keep. *Suppose:* the notes say "gridded" and the title says "Polar-Gridded". *Then:* one ground truth triple, with "Polar-Gridded", by rule 13. (Two ground truth triples that are the same [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) in one record stop step 070 until one is deleted: `annotations/README.md`, *What the pipeline checks*.)
7. **[Source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text): the shortest passage that states the fact, copied exactly.** Select it in the record's text and press **Use selection**, so it is copied exactly.
8. **A number with a unit: the object instance is the number and its unit, as written; what the number measures goes in the predicate.** *Suppose:* a record says "MODIS has a spatial resolution of 250 m." *Then:* "MODIS" HAS_SPATIAL_RESOLUTION "250 m".
9. **A qualifier that is a temporal resolution, an area the data cover, a processing level, a spatial resolution, a country, a time of day, or a target body is a ground truth triple of its own, and is taken out of the instance.** *Suppose:* a record says "This global dataset provides daily snow cover." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "snow cover", HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "daily", and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "global". *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset holds nighttime SST." *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "SST" and OBSERVED_AT_TIME_OF_DAY "nighttime". *Suppose:* a record names "the U.S. National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)". *Then:* the instance is "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)", and "National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA)" BASED_IN "U.S." is a ground truth triple too. *Suppose:* a record says "The map is related to the average atomic mass on Vesta." *Then:* the instance is "average atomic mass", and the record's dataset HAS_TARGET "Vesta" is a ground truth triple of its own.
    A word naming a kind of ground or water ("land", "ocean", "sea", "ice") stays in the instance if "the [rest] of the [word]" is true, and is an area the data cover if it is false. *Suppose:* a record says "Ocean Aerosol Optical Depth" and "Ocean Surface Temperature". *Then:* "the aerosol optical depth of the ocean" is false, because the aerosols are in the air, so the record's dataset HAS_MEASUREMENTS_OF "Aerosol Optical Depth" and HAS_SPATIAL_COVERAGE "Ocean". "The temperature of the ocean surface" is true, so "Ocean Surface Temperature" stays whole.
    A qualifier stays in the instance, as written, if it is any other kind ("sea surface salinity" stays whole), or if taking it out would give a triple that isn't true. *Suppose:* a record says "an afternoon orbit". *Then:* taking "afternoon" out would give "orbit" OBSERVED_AT_TIME_OF_DAY "afternoon", which says the orbit comes from observations made in the afternoon. That isn't true, so the instance stays "afternoon orbit".
10. **A name with its abbreviation in parentheses is one instance, as written.** *Suppose:* a record says "This Level-2 (L2) dataset". *Then:* the instance is "Level-2 (L2)", with no ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple. Write ALSO_KNOWN_AS only when the record gives the other name in words of its own; the other ground truth triples then use one of the two names (rule 13). *Suppose:* a record says "The terms 'Version 61' and 'Collection 6.1' are used interchangeably in reference to this release". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_VERSION "Version 61", and "Version 61" ALSO_KNOWN_AS "Collection 6.1".
11. **General facts: include them, unless they're about [another dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) that the record's dataset isn't made from or part of.**
    - **Include** a general fact (one true beyond this record) about something the record's dataset relates to, such as its instrument, its satellite, or its mission. *Suppose:* a record says MODIS is aboard the Aqua satellite. *Then:* a ground truth triple.
    - **Leave out** facts about another dataset, unless the record's dataset is made from it or is part of it; for example, leave them out if the other dataset is a variant of the record's dataset (the same data at another time step, resolution, or version), or is made from the record's dataset. In every case, include the fact of how the two datasets are connected. *Suppose:* a record says "This dataset is used to make dataset B. B covers 2001 to 2005." *Then:* "B" COMPUTED_FROM the record's dataset is a ground truth triple, and "2001 to 2005" is left out, because it is a fact about B, which is made from the record's dataset. *Suppose:* a record says "Dataset C was used to make this dataset. C covers 2001 to 2005." *Then:* the record's dataset COMPUTED_FROM "C" and "C" HAS_TIME_SPAN "2001 to 2005" are both ground truth triples, because the record's dataset is made from C. *Suppose:* a record's title says "8 Day", and its notes say "daily, weekly (8 day), and monthly products are available". *Then:* the record's dataset HAS_TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION "weekly (8 day)", its own (rule 13: it says more than the title's "8 Day"); the daily and monthly products are variants of the record's dataset, so they get none.
12. **A list joined by "and": one ground truth triple per item, when each item alone is a complete object.** Put one item alone after the predicate. *Suppose:* a record says the SST is "derived from the long wave IR 11 and 12 micron wavelength channels". *Then:* "long wave IR 11 micron wavelength channel" alone is a complete object, so two ground truth triples, each object with the words the items share copied onto it, in the singular, since each is now one item: "long wave IR 11 micron wavelength channel" and "long wave IR 12 micron wavelength channel". *Suppose:* a record says Aqua was launched "to study the global dynamics of the Earth atmosphere, land and oceans". *Then:* "atmosphere" alone isn't a purpose (it needs "study the global dynamics of the Earth"), so one ground truth triple keeps the whole phrase.
13. **One thing, one wording, within a record.** When a record names the same thing more than once, in different words: if one wording says more (it contains the other, is more precise, or spells out its abbreviation), use it; if they are two names that say the same, use the one the record uses most often (on a tie, the one it gives first); the other appears only in an ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple, and only if the record states one (rule 10). *Suppose:* a record says "generated by the NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)", and later "JPL acquires data from the OBPG". *Then:* both ground truth triples use "NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)", so both name it the same way. *Suppose:* a title says "4km" and the notes say "4.63 km". *Then:* "4.63 km", the more precise. **Leave out a type word that only describes the thing,** written in lowercase beside the name: write "Aqua", not "Aqua satellite", since its entity class already says it's a satellite. **Keep a type word written as part of the name,** capitalized: write "Hurricane Katrina", not "Katrina". **Keep it too when the words without it aren't a name:** write "GRaND plus Z boron loaded sensor", not "GRaND plus Z boron loaded". A wording that differs only in capitals is the same wording, not a second one. Write it as it appears where the record isn't typed all in capitals (usually the notes): "Vesta", not the all-capitals title's "VESTA". If it appears only in all capitals, write it as written. (This sentence is only in these guidelines, not in the model's copy of the rules: the evaluation ignores capitals, so it changes no score, and the model has one rule less to follow.) To see that two wordings name the same thing, use clues anywhere in the record, and what you know about the words (e.g. that MID-IR means mid-infrared); but never add a fact the record doesn't state, and write only a wording the record itself uses for that thing, after any qualifiers rule 9 takes out.
14. **A processing step: output_i = method_i(input_i).** For each processing step i the record describes, find its output_i, method_i, and input_i, and write at most two ground truth triples: output_i COMPUTED_FROM input_i, and output_i PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM method_i. Write each only if the record names both of its parts. Steps chain: one step's output can be the next step's input. *Suppose:* a record says a dataset holds "snow cover detected using NDSI and a series of screens" and "the NDSI is derived from radiance data". *Then:* step 1 is NDSI = method_1(radiance data), with method_1 not named, which gives "NDSI" COMPUTED_FROM "radiance data". Step 2 is the record's dataset = screens(NDSI), which gives *the record's dataset* COMPUTED_FROM "NDSI" and *the record's dataset* PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "a series of screens".
    A word saying only that something was done to the data ("corrected", "calibrated", "detrended") does not name a method. *Suppose:* a record says "the radiances were calibrated, then cloud-cleared using the CCR algorithm". *Then:* "calibrated" names no method, so it gives no PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM triple. "the CCR algorithm" does name one, so that step gives PRODUCED_USING_ALGORITHM "the CCR algorithm".

### Examples

*Two facts in one sentence, and an opinion word.* *Suppose:* a record's text says "The NASA Technical Standards Program provides access to NASA developed standards and handbooks. … The idea is to make it simpler for technical people to access great NASA technical information quickly and conveniently." *Then:* "standards and handbooks" are two things, so two ground truth triples: "NASA Technical Standards Program" (Organization) PROVIDES_ACCESS_TO_DOCUMENT "NASA developed standards" (Document), and the same with "NASA developed handbooks". The purpose is written without "great". (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv`, record `3122be4c-b9d0-4cc9-b6d3-9b96c6e743da`.)

*The subject isn't always the title.* *Suppose:* a record titled "ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P RSI 1/2/3 COMET ESCORT 4 1149 V1.0" says "This is a Rosetta Radio Science data set, collected during the COMET ESCORT 4 phase 2015-10-22 to 2015-12-31." *Then:* the facts about [the record's dataset](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) have the title as their [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): … FROM_MISSION "Rosetta" (Mission), … HAS_DISCIPLINE "Radio Science" (Discipline), … HAS_MISSION_PHASE "COMET ESCORT 4" (MissionPhase). But the dates belong to the phase, not to the record's dataset: "COMET ESCORT 4" (MissionPhase) HAS_TIME_SPAN "2015-10-22 to 2015-12-31" (Time). (From `annotations/ground_truth/batch_000.csv`, record `cfd6ec3f-1e63-4a77-bc3f-007d45f7e2fc`.)

## The describes triple

Every record has one [classed triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) that no text states, written by code: the record's id DESCRIBES its title. Your only part in it is its [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas), the [describes class](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples): the entity class of the thing the title names (e.g. Dataset, WebTool). Choose it in the tool's field *Object's entity class* of the [describes triple](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples). `X` means none is chosen yet.

## Coining an entity class or predicate

- **Coin one only when none of the [ground truth schema](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)'s fits.** Check the tool's list as you type, and **Help → Ground truth schema** (in the record's strip), which shows every one at a glance: a near-synonym may already exist.
- **Spell it in the ground truth schema's style:** an [entity class](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas) in CamelCase (`SpaceMission`), a predicate in UPPER_SNAKE_CASE (`PART_OF_MISSION`), one word each.
- **Give it a one-line definition at once:** press the button under its ✖, in the tool. Until then the model sees it without a definition, the tool marks it ✖, and step 070 won't evaluate a [finished record](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) that uses it.
- **To rename one, or merge two that mean the same thing:** the tool's **Help → Ground truth schema**, then **Edit definitions, rename, or merge…**, then **[Rename or merge…](../docs/terminology.md#3-schemas)** (`annotations/README.md`, *The ground truth schema*).

## Finishing a record

- **Tick *All facts extracted*** only when every fact the record states is there. Only [finished records](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) are extracted (step 060) and evaluated (step 070).
- **A record that states no facts:** delete every [ground truth triple](../docs/terminology.md#4-ground-truth-and-samples) of the record; the tool then keeps one row of the file with only the record's id, which means "annotated, states no facts". Still tick *All facts extracted*.
- **Fix what the tool marks:** ✖ must be fixed (e.g. a [source text](../docs/terminology.md#1-records-and-their-text) that isn't in the record's text); ⚑ is worth a look (e.g. a [subject instance](../docs/terminology.md#2-triples) that isn't in the text, which may be a typo or a deliberate rewording).
- **If a ground truth triple isn't connected to the catalog entry through the others,** look for the fact that connects it; add it only if the record states it. The tool marks such triples ⚑.
- **Commit the file to Git** (`annotations/ground_truth/batch_<NNN>.csv`).

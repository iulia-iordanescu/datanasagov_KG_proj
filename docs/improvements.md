# Improvements list

The changes to make to the pipeline itself: what to change, why, and where the idea came from. Not to be confused with the upkeep (`upkeep.md`): what a person running the pipeline does, and when. An item that is done is deleted here, and the commit that does it says so; its *From:* credit moves first ([`upkeep.md`](upkeep.md#when-an-item-of-the-improvements-list-is-done)). Measured results that an item rests on are in [`findings.md`](findings.md).

## The whole pipeline

- **Repeat runs, to measure run-to-run noise.** A setting that skips the saved answers, so that a step can be run again with exactly the same settings. One run isn't a baseline: two runs of step 040 with the same settings, differing only by one rule of the prompt (rule 8 of *What counts as a fact*), spelled 41 of the 63 entity classes and 67 of the 126 predicates the same. From: [`findings.md`](findings.md), 2026-10-12. Extended on 2026-11-01: **for the papers, 5 repeats of the baseline** (3 for each change). Why: the standard deviation of *k* repeats has *k* − 1 degrees of freedom, so from 3 repeats it is known only to within a factor of about 6 upward (95% interval 0.52 to 6.3 times the value measured), from 5 to within about 3 (0.60 to 2.9 times), by the chi-square interval for a standard deviation (NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, [section 1.3.5.8](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda358.htm)); the decision rule averages each recipe's repeats, so a baseline of 5 and a change of 3 compare fairly (`helpers/compare_runs.md`, *Did a change help?*). Smallest version: repeats 4 and 5 of the baseline recipe after the 3 planned, each followed by `py 070_evaluate/run.py`. Effort: an hour at the keyboard; the runs take longer. Model cost: 2 × the finished records' extractions. Needs the models before 2026-11-20: yes. From (extension): the research of 2026-10-31 on repeated runs, section 4.
- **Repeats that LiteLLM may answer from its own cache (2026-10-31).** On 2026-10-31, `py helpers/check_effort.py`, run through NASA's LiteLLM with `claude-opus-5.5`, sent each setting the same request 3 times and got exactly the same completion-token count each time: 717, 717, 717 (no effort sent); 814, 814, 814 (`low`); 802, 802, 802 (`high`), from a model sent no temperature and with adaptive thinking. That suggests the proxy answers a repeated identical request from a cache of its own ([LiteLLM caching](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/caching): "The second response is served from the cache", with the header `x-litellm-cache-key`). If so, the runs of a recipe's repeats (setting `repeat`) are copies of one run, and their spread says nothing about the model's own variation. Done on 2026-10-31: `py helpers/check_answer_cache.py` (4 tiny paid calls, asked about before sending; `running_on_nasa_laptop.md`, *Checking LiteLLM's answer cache*) sends one question twice as it is and twice with LiteLLM's per-request control `{"cache": {"no-cache": true, "no-store": true}}` ([LiteLLM cache controls](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/caching_controls)), and decides by a rule printed before the calls: "LiteLLM answers from its cache" if the second plain call carries `x-litellm-cache-key` or returns a byte-identical answer with identical usage, while the no-cache calls differ from each other or carry no `x-litellm-cache-key`; "no answer cache seen" if no call carries the header and the two plain answers differ; "not shown" otherwise, with the reason; and separately whether the proxy accepted the control (no HTTP 4xx). The bypass itself is written (`common/common_helpers/llm.py`, `ANSWER_CACHE_CONTROL`): with `LITELLM_BYPASS_ANSWER_CACHE=1` in `.env`, every LiteLLM request of 040, 050, 060, 070 and the checks carries the control, never one through Ask Sage; the words the model gets, the cache keys and the fingerprints don't change; each run's record (`answer_cache`) and report (*Model calls*) say "LiteLLM answer cache: bypassed" or "not bypassed", and 070's recipe carries it, so `py helpers/compare_runs.py` lists runs with and without it under *What differs*. It is off by default because LiteLLM's docs don't say whether a proxy with its cache off accepts the control, so it is switched on by the check's evidence. Done on 2026-11-01: the check said "LiteLLM answers from its cache", the proxy accepted the control, and `LITELLM_BYPASS_ANSWER_CACHE=1` is in the NASA laptop's `.env` since that day (`findings.md`, 2026-11-01); no repeat had been run through LiteLLM before then. From: `findings.md`, 2026-10-31 (`check_effort.py`'s counts).

- **An entity class hierarchy for the schemas:** the ground truth schema is flat (no entity class is a kind of another). An umbrella entity class with subclasses is standard in knowledge graph schemas (subClassOf in W3C, "RDF Schema 1.1", Recommendation, 25 February 2014, [page](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-schema/)); for people and groups it has a standard name, Agent: FOAF puts Person, Organization and Group under it (Brickley and Miller, "FOAF Vocabulary Specification 0.99", 14 January 2014, [page](http://xmlns.com/foaf/spec/)), and PROV-O puts Person, Organization and SoftwareAgent under it (W3C, "PROV-O: The PROV Ontology", Recommendation, 30 April 2013, [page](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). For what carries instruments it has one too, a platform: W3C's sensor ontology says "A Platform is an entity [a thing] that hosts other entities [things], particularly Sensors, Actuators, Samplers, and other Platforms" (W3C, "Semantic Sensor Network Ontology", [page](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-ssn/)), and NASA's GCMD Platforms keywords group space-, air-, water- and land-based platforms (checked on 2026-10-31; Q4 and Q25 of annotations/questions_for_experts.md no longer ask it). With it, a pin could say any -> Agent instead of listing Organization and Person (DEVELOPED_BY_PARTY, SPONSORED_BY_PARTY), and the graph could answer questions across the kinds. It touches the schema reader and its file format, the pin check, step 040's induced schema, and step 070's translation table, so it is a design change for after the first run. Some entity classes only ever appear as the object of one predicate, and their definitions just restate it: Purpose (HAS_PURPOSE), Task (RESPONSIBLE_FOR), Role (SERVES_AS), ProcessingLevel (HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL), SpatialRepresentation (HAS_SPATIAL_REPRESENTATION). They cause no disagreement today, unlike Value and Time, which were merged for that reason on 2026-10-20; with a hierarchy they could sit under one umbrella entity class (e.g. Label). Evidence that an induced schema is often finer than one written by people, so that a hierarchy would let the two meet: in TRACE-KG (Abolhasani et al., arXiv:2604.03496v3), 48.1% of the entity classes of their benchmark's hand-built schemas (weighted by use, all scopes together) were matched only by a narrower entity class of the induced schema, against 50.2% matched by an equivalent one (Table 2, *Combined*), and of its accepted matches for entity classes, 13.8% were best at the coarsest level of its hierarchy (appendix C.2, Table 6). From: 2026-10-20; TRACE-KG (Abolhasani et al., arXiv:2604.03496v3), for the evidence.

Pairing is not affected: "1 km" and "1 km2" never pair. From: 2026-10-24.
- **Ideas from TRACE-KG not to adopt, and why** (Abolhasani et al., arXiv:2604.03496v3):
  - *Prefer recall over precision*, inferred facts included: its extraction prompts say "Prefer recall over precision" (`TKG_Prompts.py`, lines 28 and 883) and keep a fact rated "Plausible inference; partial support (still include)" (line 77). That conflicts with guidelines 1 and 7 of *What counts as a fact*, "Only what the record states" and "Not uncertain facts".
  - Subject instances and object instances in the model's words: its prompt has the model name each thing in a short, reusable form, which may not occur in the text as written (line 865). That conflicts with rule 11 of *Writing a classed triple* (the record's own words, as written) and with the source text check (`common/common_helpers/validate.py`).
  - A qualifier dictionary on each edge (section 4.4, appendix B.5): there, a "qualifier" is a condition a triple holds under (when, where, how certain), kept with the edge, not a word inside a subject or object instance as in this project (see *qualifier* in `terminology.md`). Its "UncertaintyQualifier" keeps hedged facts, against guideline 7, "Not uncertain facts". Their ablation found qualifiers mattered most on their benchmark, judged by a model (appendix C.4).

- **Rule 19 can lose which fact a qualifier went with (decided on 2026-10-30: option 2).** Rule 19 of *Writing a classed triple* (annotations/guidelines.md): "A qualifier that is a temporal resolution, an area the data cover, a processing level, a spatial resolution, a country, a time of day, or a target body is a ground truth triple of its own, and is taken out of the instance." Setup: a ground truth triple S P O, where the object instance O contains a qualifier q ("Earth"). Rule 19 takes q out of O and writes a new ground truth triple S Q q (e.g. X HAS_TARGET "Earth"). S, O and O′ are subject instances or object instances; q becomes the object instance of the new ground truth triple; P, P′ and Q are predicates of the ground truth schema. The problem: S Q q says q is about S, but in the record q was only about O. That goes wrong when S has another ground truth triple S P′ O′ whose object instance O′ did not contain q. Two forms, depending on O′:

- **The decision rule for "did a change help" (decided on 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01; done).** One run of a change against one baseline run, or a number read against the width of the baseline's repeats, has no stated chance of a wrong call, and among the about 30 numbers of a comparison some differ by chance. Now `py helpers/compare_runs.py --baseline <run ids> --changed <run ids>` applies one rule fixed before the runs: partial F1 alone decides (the deciding metric: *Untranslatable occurrences left out of precision*, below), the other numbers are shown, not tested. Each record's counts are averaged over each recipe's repeats, *D* is the difference of the two recipes' pooled partial F1, and an exact paired permutation (sign-flip) test over records gives a two-sided *p*: every one of the 2^*n* swaps of the two recipes' counts on some of the *n* records up to 20 records, 100,000 random swaps from a fixed seed above. The verdict is *helped* (*D* > 0 and *p* ≤ 0.05), *worse* (*D* < 0 and *p* ≤ 0.05) or *can't tell apart*. The report also gives the smallest *p* the test can give on its records, 1 ÷ 2^(*n* − 1), so below 6 records it says that no verdict but *can't tell apart* is possible. It stops on runs evaluated under different evaluation versions, translated in different ways, or not all on the same tuning records. Home: `helpers/compare_runs.md`, *Did a change help?*; tests: `tests/test_evaluate_pairing.py`, *DecisionRule*. Not built: the research's other parts (a run-noise comparison beside *D*, a "not worse" rule for a change kept for other reasons). ## Considered and not changed

Proposals that were looked at and decided against, or decisions to keep something as it is, so that nothing here is "fixed" back by accident. Read this before proposing a change to a rule, the ground truth, a prompt or a metric. Each entry: what was proposed, what was decided, why, when, and where it lives. A decision with a home of its own elsewhere is listed here with a pointer.

**Annotation guidelines and ground truth** (`annotations/guidelines.md`; rule numbers as of 2026-10-30)

- **Capitals, spacing, hyphens and punctuation sentences of rule 23 stay out of the model's copy of the rules** (`common/common_prompts/extraction_rules.txt`). Decided when the capitals sentences were written (commit ef16bc5); copied into the model's copy by mistake on 2026-10-30 (commit 1f82845, applying audit finding R5) and restored the same day. Why: step 070 ignores capitals, spacing, punctuation and dashes when pairing, so they change no score, and the model has fewer rules to follow. Home: rule 23's note.
- **Title-case ties in rule 23** (considered on 2026-10-30): treating a title in title case like one in all capitals, which would change four instances ("Swath" → "swath", "Weekly" → "weekly", "Daily" → "daily" twice). Not adopted: step 070's pairing ignores case, and step 080 will normalize names, so the choice changes neither scores nor the graph.
- **Different wordings of one thing across records** ("ROSETTA-ORBITER" in two records, "Rosetta Orbiter" in another; 2026-10-30): rule 23 works within a record only. Joining them is step 080's job (*Joining subject instances and object instances that name the same thing into one node*, under 080).
- **Rule 23's "first wording wins" for small differences** (approved, then withdrawn on 2026-10-30 before it was applied): it would have made an all-capitals title win ("VESTA" over "Vesta"), against the capitals sentence. Rule 23 keeps "where the record isn't typed all in capitals … the form used most often (on a tie, the first)".
- **Keeping a qualifier inside an object that describes** ("rule 19 doesn't apply to it", e.g. Aqua's purpose keeping "Earth"; rejected on 2026-10-27): every triple follows the same rules; a small exception would slip past the model; keeping qualifiers in some instances and out of others is the inconsistency a knowledge graph must avoid. Not reopened by rule 19's *A country* of 2026-10-31, which keeps a country in the instance only when the words it qualifies name no Person or Organization, in an instance of any entity class. Home: rule 19, and *Rule 19 can lose which fact a qualifier went with* (050), which also records options 1 and 3 as not adopted and option 4 as future work.
- **Rule 22's "when each item alone is a complete object"** (dropped on 2026-10-30): a judgment call, not a test. Rule 22 now splits every list, with only the collective-"and" and period-or-range exceptions. Still not adopted after 2026-10-31, when rule 22's describing words got the question "can one of the things they describe have all the describing words at once?": that question asks about one item and the words, which the annotator checks against what the record means; "a complete object" asked whether an item could stand alone, which no one can check the same way.
- **The seven-word collective-"and" test** ("between", "continuity", "combined", "merged", "ratio", "difference", "compared"; approved, then not written on 2026-10-30): it would have kept three correctly split lists whole ("Normalized Difference Snow Index (NDSI) and a series of screens", "between … NASA and … CONAE", "Combined Terra and Aqua"). Replaced by the five nouns of rule 22's exception.
- **Searching the whole catalog to shape a rule** (considered on 2026-10-30, for the collective-"and" nouns): not done. The catalog includes the ground truth candidates pool, and with it the held-out records the final scores come from; a rule shaped by their text would make those scores overstated. Any such search must leave out the whole pool (search the induction candidates only). The collective-"and" exception became a definition instead (050, *Audit of the rules and the ground truth*).
- **Inference by confidence** ("when we are 97% sure"; considered on 2026-10-30): no one can check a confidence, two annotators (or the annotator and the model) would differ, and an inferred fact has no source text. Instead, rule 1 of *What counts as a fact* allows a fact only through a written test met by the record's own statements (SUPERSEDES, comparisons).
- **The model's own knowledge in the ground truth or extraction** (considered on 2026-10-30): kept out, so that step 070 measures how well the pipeline reads the record. Home for using it: *Knowledge enrichment from the model, verified and labelled* (080). One exception since 2026-10-31: the entity class, and only the entity class, of a name an official NASA list names for the record's own mission or platform (rule 1's *Names on an official NASA list*; the model's copy, *Names you recognize*), because a person checks it against the list, not against what the model knows.

**Evaluation and pipeline**

- **An AI model as a third pair level** (judging whether two differently worded triples state the same fact): not used; every run would cost model calls, and a model tends to favor output like its own. A person checks a sample instead. Home: `070_evaluate/metrics/pairs.md`, *The same fact in different words*.
- **Inducing the schema from a larger or more varied sample, to reach the 40 facts out of reach** (considered on 2026-10-30): not done; 12 of the 13 ground truth records come from the maintainers the schema was induced from, step 040's saturation curve is nearly flat for predicates (2.8 added by the last 25 of 150 texts), and 69 of the 104 induced predicates translate to (none) because their meaning splits. Conditional rows were built instead (*Translation that depends on the entity classes*, 070).
- **Re-attaching a predicate to another subject in the translation table** (considered on 2026-11-01): not done. A fact the two schemas attach to different subjects (a resolution stated of a variable in the current schema, of the dataset in the ground truth) never pairs; EDOAL can express such a correspondence by a path, but a row translates a predicate in place, and no column can change that. Kept as a known limit instead. Home: `070_evaluate/070_evaluate.md`, *Known limits*.
- **Credit for a broader or narrower translation in the metrics** (considered on 2026-11-01): not done. The extraction benchmarks score a triple only after canonicalization to one target or none (EDC; Text2KGBench), and the Ontology Alignment Evaluation Initiative scores non-equivalence correspondences by hand or by relaxed measures, so no standard automatic credit exists; step 070 counts by match kind instead and leaves every formula as it is. Home: `docs/terminology.md`, *match*.
- **Rerunning step 040 casually:** a new induced schema would need the translation table checked again. Home: `docs/upkeep.md`, *Translation table*.
- **The fixed numbers of step 070** (`FEW_RECORDS`, `Z` and the others) are fixed on purpose. Home: 070's guide.
- **`splits.json` is written once and kept**, so a re-harvest doesn't reorder the pool. Home: 030's guide.
- **Prompt caching:** step 060 marks its system block for caching; step 040's requests are not cached on purpose (small saving). Home: `docs/findings.md`, 2026-10-12.

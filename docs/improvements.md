- **An entity class hierarchy for the schemas:** the ground truth schema is flat (no entity class is a kind of another). An umbrella entity class with subclasses is standard in knowledge graph schemas (subClassOf in W3C, "RDF Schema 1.1", Recommendation, 25 February 2014, [page](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-schema/)); for people and groups it has a standard name, Agent: FOAF puts Person, Organization and Group under it (Brickley and Miller, "FOAF Vocabulary Specification 0.99", 14 January 2014, [page](http://xmlns.com/foaf/spec/)), and PROV-O puts Person, Organization and SoftwareAgent under it (W3C, "PROV-O: The PROV Ontology", Recommendation, 30 April 2013, [page](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)). For what carries instruments it has one too, a platform: W3C's sensor ontology says "A Platform is an entity [a thing] that hosts other entities [things], particularly Sensors, Actuators, Samplers, and other Platforms" (W3C, "Semantic Sensor Network Ontology", [page](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-ssn/)), and NASA's GCMD Platforms keywords group space-, air-, water- and land-based platforms (checked on 2026-10-31; Q4 and Q25 of annotations/questions_for_experts.md no longer ask it). With it, a pin could say any -> Agent instead of listing Organization and Person (DEVELOPED_BY_PARTY, SPONSORED_BY_PARTY), and the graph could answer questions across the kinds. It touches the schema reader and its file format, the pin check, step 040's induced schema, and step 070's translation table, so it is a design change for after the first run. Some entity classes only ever appear as the object of one predicate, and their definitions just restate it: Purpose (HAS_PURPOSE), Task (RESPONSIBLE_FOR), Role (SERVES_AS), ProcessingLevel (HAS_PROCESSING_LEVEL), SpatialRepresentation (HAS_SPATIAL_REPRESENTATION). They cause no disagreement today, unlike Value and Time, which were merged for that reason on 2026-10-20; with a hierarchy they could sit under one umbrella entity class (e.g. Label). Evidence that an induced schema is often finer than one written by people, so that a hierarchy would let the two meet: in TRACE-KG (Abolhasani et al., arXiv:2604.03496v3), 48.1% of the entity classes of their benchmark's hand-built schemas (weighted by use, all scopes together) were matched only by a narrower entity class of the induced schema, against 50.2% matched by an equivalent one (Table 2, *Combined*), and of its accepted matches for entity classes, 13.8% were best at the coarsest level of its hierarchy (appendix C.2, Table 6). From: 2026-10-20; TRACE-KG (Abolhasani et al., arXiv:2604.03496v3), for the evidence.

  - *Prefer recall over precision*, inferred facts included: its extraction prompts say "Prefer recall over precision" (`TKG_Prompts.py`, lines 28 and 883) and keep a fact rated "Plausible inference; partial support (still include)" (line 77). That conflicts with guidelines 1 and 7 of *What counts as a fact*, "Only what the record states" and "Not uncertain facts".
  - Subject instances and object instances in the model's words: its prompt has the model name each thing in a short, reusable form, which may not occur in the text as written (line 865). That conflicts with rule 11 of *Writing a classed triple* (the record's own words, as written) and with the source text check (`common/common_helpers/validate.py`).
  - A qualifier dictionary on each edge (section 4.4, appendix B.5): there, a "qualifier" is a condition a triple holds under (when, where, how certain), kept with the edge, not a word inside a subject or object instance as in this project (see *qualifier* in `terminology.md`). Its "UncertaintyQualifier" keeps hedged facts, against guideline 7, "Not uncertain facts". Their ablation found qualifiers mattered most on their benchmark, judged by a model (appendix C.4).
  - Never more than one edge between the same subject and object instances: its prompt says to merge them all (line 1211). That contradicts its own section 4.5, which merges only two edges between the same things that mean the same, and would drop distinct facts. Suppose a record says NASA developed and sponsored a mission; then DEVELOPED_BY_PARTY and SPONSORED_BY_PARTY (ground truth schema) join the same two instances, and one would be lost.
  - Its "Leakage" metric, the overlap of 4-word runs between the text and the names of things (section 5.1, appendix C.1), counts copying the record's words as a fault: the opposite of rule 11 of *Writing a classed triple*.
  - A model judge, as the evaluation, of whether a fact "can be supported or inferred" from the graph (`TKG_Experiment_1.py`, line 1359; its section 5.1 says the judge allows "no inferred edges"): inferred facts would count as found, and the judge's own errors go unmeasured. Step 070 compares with a ground truth a person checked.
  - A model's own confidence as a gate (appendices C.2 and D: below 0.88 goes to a person): the paper gives no evidence that it is calibrated. A person checks every row of the translation table instead.

- **Rule 19 can lose which fact a qualifier went with (decided on 2026-10-30: option 2).** Rule 19 of *Writing a classed triple* (annotations/guidelines.md): "A qualifier that is a temporal resolution, an area the data cover, a processing level, a spatial resolution, a country, a time of day, or a target body is a ground truth triple of its own, and is taken out of the instance." Setup: a ground truth triple S P O, where the object instance O contains a qualifier q ("Earth"). Rule 19 takes q out of O and writes a new ground truth triple S Q q (e.g. X HAS_TARGET "Earth"). S, O and O′ are subject instances or object instances; q becomes the object instance of the new ground truth triple; P, P′ and Q are predicates of the ground truth schema. The problem: S Q q says q is about S, but in the record q was only about O. That goes wrong when S has another ground truth triple S P′ O′ whose object instance O′ did not contain q. Two forms, depending on O′:

  | Form | O′ contains | Result | What's lost |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | A | another qualifier q′ of the same kind ("Mars") | S Q q and S Q q′ | which of q and q′ goes with which object instance |
  | B | no qualifier of that kind ("solar wind") | S Q q only | that q does not go with O′ |

  No problem when every other object instance of S that a qualifier of this kind could describe also contained q (Aqua: "study the global dynamics of the Earth atmosphere, land and oceans": "Earth" goes with all three). *Suppose:* a record says X was launched "to study the Earth atmosphere and the Mars surface". *Then:* after rule 19, X HAS_TARGET "Earth" and X HAS_TARGET "Mars", and nothing says which goes with which purpose (form A). *Suppose:* a record says X was launched "to study the Earth atmosphere and the solar wind". *Then:* X HAS_TARGET "Earth" reads as covering the solar wind too (form B). From: 2026-10-11.
- **One written form for every value:** every exported value is written in one form, whatever spelling the record used: a number, a space, and the unit, as the SI brochure prescribes (e.g. 500 m, never 500m; 4.63 km; the only exceptions are °, ′ and ″ of a plane angle, written with no space). Matching (070, the annotation tool) already treats both spellings as the same, and, since 2026-10-24, every spelling of one quantity ("1 kilometer (km)", "1 km", "1000 m": `common/common_helpers/text_match.py`, `QUANTITY_UNITS`, the one table to write the form from); the graph must not keep both. From: 2026-10-20; BIPM, *The International System of Units (SI)*, 9th edition (2019), section 5.4.3.
- **A fact many records state:** one edge per extracted triple, or one edge listing the records it came from. Either way, keep which records each edge came from. From: 2026-10-06.
- **Joining subject instances and object instances that name the same thing into one node** beyond the lists: similar spelling plus shared neighbors (two spacecraft names spelled alike that carry the same instrument), grouped into connected components, the longest name kept; generic words ("Data", "Product", "Level") never count as similar. Which subject instances and object instances to join across records, and which to keep as one node per record: specific named things join (one MODIS node, one Aqua, one NASA), since they are important and specific. Generic descriptions don't: "L2 pixels" (record #4, pool position 4), "radiance data" and "a series of screens" (pool position 2) each mean that record's own data or method, so one shared "L2 pixels" node would wrongly connect unrelated datasets. Matching words alone can't tell the two kinds apart, so 080 needs a test for it: e.g. a proper name or an acronym against a common noun phrase, or a model's judgment checked on a sample. Organizations inside organizations: the ground truth keeps an organization's name whole, as written (e.g. "NASA Ocean Biology Processing Group (OBPG)", pool position 4), because "NASA" there says whose group it is, while in "NASA Technical Standards Program" (pool position 0) it is part of the name, and wording alone can't tell the two apart. So 080 should add part-of edges (OBPG part of NASA) from a known list of NASA's organizations, not from the words, so that such groups connect to the one NASA node. Groups named with a person: since 2026-10-30, rule 22 of the annotation guidelines splits "Peter Minnett and his team" (pool position 4) and drops "his team", so the ground truth has a "Peter Minnett" node that can join a "Peter Minnett" node from another record. Same name, different things: NASA often gives a mission and its spacecraft the same name (Dawn, Rosetta, Aqua). A Mission node and a Spacecraft node with the same name must stay two nodes, never joined because the names match. They may be linked (the spacecraft belongs to the mission), but only from a record that states it. What never to join, from TRACE-KG's prompt for joining (`TKG_Prompts.py`, lines 247–250): a thing and the entity class it belongs to ("Aqua" and "satellite"), a kind and a narrower kind ("sea surface temperature" and "skin sea surface temperature"), and a quantity and a measurement of it ("sea surface temperature" and "a sea surface temperature of 25 °C"). And never replace a node's words from the record with a model's: in TRACE-KG's audit, 16 of 249 joining actions (6.4%) were wrong changes to a thing's name, description, or type hint (appendix B.4.1; appendix C.3, Table 8). From: Negro, Futia, Kůs, and Montagna, *Knowledge Graphs and LLMs in Action* (Manning, 2026), section 6.1.4; 2026-10-19; 2026-10-22; TRACE-KG (Abolhasani et al., arXiv:2604.03496v3), for what never to join and for keeping the record's words. An example found on 2026-10-30: "ROSETTA-ORBITER" in two Rosetta records and "Rosetta Orbiter" in the ALICE record ("ROSETTA-ORBITER 67P/CAL ALICE 2 ESC1 V1.0"); rule 23 of *Writing a classed triple* picks one wording within a record only, so different records can still differ, and joining them is this item's job.
- **Checks of the graph:** how well nodes were joined (precision and recall of the joining), duplicate nodes, and a person checking a sample of edges. ## Considered and not changed

Proposals that were looked at and decided against, or decisions to keep something as it is, so that nothing here is "fixed" back by accident. Read this before proposing a change to a rule, the ground truth, a prompt or a metric. Each entry: what was proposed, what was decided, why, when, and where it lives. A decision with a home of its own elsewhere is listed here with a pointer.

**Annotation guidelines and ground truth** (`annotations/guidelines.md`; rule numbers as of 2026-10-30)

- **Capitals, spacing, hyphens and punctuation sentences of rule 23 stay out of the model's copy of the rules** (`common/common_prompts/extraction_rules.txt`). Decided when the capitals sentences were written (commit ef16bc5); copied into the model's copy by mistake on 2026-10-30 (commit 1f82845, applying audit finding R5) and restored the same day. Why: step 070 ignores capitals, spacing, punctuation and dashes when pairing, so they change no score, and the model has fewer rules to follow. Home: rule 23's note.
- **Title-case ties in rule 23** (considered on 2026-10-30): treating a title in title case like one in all capitals, which would change four instances ("Swath" → "swath", "Weekly" → "weekly", "Daily" → "daily" twice). Not adopted: step 070's pairing ignores case, and step 080 will normalize names, so the choice changes neither scores nor the graph.
- **Different wordings of one thing across records** ("ROSETTA-ORBITER" in two records, "Rosetta Orbiter" in another; 2026-10-30): rule 23 works within a record only. Joining them is step 080's job (*Joining subject instances and object instances that name the same thing into one node*, under 080).
- **Rule 23's "first wording wins" for small differences** (approved, then withdrawn on 2026-10-30 before it was applied): it would have made an all-capitals title win ("VESTA" over "Vesta"), against the capitals sentence. Rule 23 keeps "where the record isn't typed all in capitals … the form used most often (on a tie, the first)".
- **Keeping a qualifier inside an object that describes** ("rule 19 doesn't apply to it", e.g. Aqua's purpose keeping "Earth"; rejected on 2026-10-27): every triple follows the same rules; a small exception would slip past the model; keeping qualifiers in some instances and out of others is the inconsistency a knowledge graph must avoid. Home: rule 19, and *Rule 19 can lose which fact a qualifier went with* (050), which also records options 1 and 3 as not adopted and option 4 as future work.
- **Rule 22's "when each item alone is a complete object"** (dropped on 2026-10-30): a judgment call, not a test. Rule 22 now splits every list, with only the collective-"and" and period-or-range exceptions.
- **The seven-word collective-"and" test** ("between", "continuity", "combined", "merged", "ratio", "difference", "compared"; approved, then not written on 2026-10-30): it would have kept three correctly split lists whole ("Normalized Difference Snow Index (NDSI) and a series of screens", "between … NASA and … CONAE", "Combined Terra and Aqua"). The catalog includes the ground truth candidates pool, and with it the held-out records the final scores come from; a rule shaped by their text would make those scores overstated. Any such search must leave out the whole pool (search the induction candidates only). The collective-"and" exception became a definition instead (050, *Audit of the rules and the ground truth*).
- **Inference by confidence** ("when we are 97% sure"; considered on 2026-10-30): no one can check a confidence, two annotators (or the annotator and the model) would differ, and an inferred fact has no source text. Instead, rule 1 of *What counts as a fact* allows a fact only through a written test met by the record's own statements (SUPERSEDES, comparisons).
